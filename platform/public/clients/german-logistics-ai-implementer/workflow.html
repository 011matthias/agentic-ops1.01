Onboarding playbook

How I take a tenant from sold to live

Same playbook each time. Tweaks per tenant where they matter, repetition where they don't.

The six steps

STEP 01
Kickoff intake (async, 1 to 2 hours)
Collect tenant config: branding assets, IMAP and SMTP, module flags, business rules, escalation contacts, sender display name. Standard form, fillable in the tenant's own time. The form is the one on the Get started page.
↓
STEP 02
Tenant scaffold (2 to 3 hours)
Set up the tenant in your AI-OS: branding applied, module flags set, credentials wired. Smoke-test that the inbound channel reaches the configured queue. The tenant doesn't need to be in the room for this part.
↓
STEP 03
Workflow customization (2 to 4 hours)
Adapt prompts and templates to the tenant's voice and terminology. Run 5 to 10 representative tickets through dry-run, tune confidence thresholds. Document the per-tenant escalation boundary.
↓
STEP 04
Onboarding call in German (60 to 90 minutes)
Walkthrough, IMAP and SMTP live test, one real test ticket end to end, Q&A. Recording on request. Written summary in German sent within 24 hours.
↓
STEP 05
First-weeks support (~2 hours per week, 2 to 4 weeks)
Slack or email channel I'm on. Daily error-log check for the first 2 weeks. Weekly summary email: what triggered, what was auto-handled, what needed human attention, what got tuned. Tune anything that surfaces.
↓
STEP 06
30-day quality check (1 hour)
Review what triggered the first 30 days, what was auto-handled, what needed human attention, what the model got wrong. Decide together whether the bonus criteria are met. This is also where we lock in lessons that go into the playbook for tenant N+1.

Total per tenant

8 to 15 hours per tenant, depending on tenant complexity. Matches the range you posted. Simpler tenants (clean credentials, narrow module flags, light terminology tuning) land near the bottom of the range. Tenants with thornier escalation rules or unusual sender setups sit closer to 15.

Why the same playbook each time

The repeatability is the point. By tenant 3 the kickoff intake is a 30-minute call instead of 90, and the tuning round is shorter because the templates have already been hardened against the first tenants' edge cases. Reusable diffs, not bespoke rebuilds.

What flexes per tenant

  • The dry-run set. A logistics tenant doing last-mile parcel has different representative tickets than one running freight-forwarding. The dry-run set is built from their actual recent tickets, not a generic sample.
  • The escalation boundary. Where the model hands off depends on what the tenant's human team is set up to absorb. Configured per tenant, in writing.
  • The 30-day criteria. The bonus criteria need to be specific to the tenant's volume and ticket mix. We'd set those together at the end of step 03, not after the fact.

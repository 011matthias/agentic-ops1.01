Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers about scope, gaps, logistics, and pricing. If your question isn't here, the onboarding form has a free-text field for anything else.

Scope and Approach

How is this different from hiring a marketing generalist? ▼

A marketing generalist manages campaigns, writes copy, runs ads, and does pipeline work manually. I build the infrastructure that automates the pipeline work so the generalist (or your team) can focus on strategy and creative.

The output is different too. A generalist gives you hours of labor. I give you automation that runs 24/7 after it's built. When the engagement ends, the automation keeps running. A generalist's output stops when they stop working.

Can you handle the marketing strategy side too? ▼

No, and I think that's actually better for you. Marketing strategy needs someone who understands your market, your positioning, and your competitive landscape. That's domain expertise I don't have for your specific industry.

What I can do is make your strategist's decisions execute faster. They decide which audience to target, what messaging to use, and which channels to prioritize. I build the automation that runs those decisions at scale without manual intervention.

What if we already have some automation running? ▼

Phase 1 starts with a stack audit specifically to map what's already automated. If you have Make.com scenarios or n8n workflows running, I'll review them, document what they do, and build the new pipeline to work alongside them rather than replacing something that's already working.

If existing automation is doing part of the job but has gaps (e.g., capture works but there's no scoring), I'll extend rather than rebuild.

Do we own everything you build? ▼

Yes. Make.com scenarios live in your account. n8n workflows run on your infrastructure. Google Sheets are in your Drive. Documentation is in files you control. Everything exports as JSON if you want backups or want to move to a different platform later.

There's no proprietary tooling, no vendor lock-in, and no "you can only use this if you keep paying me." The automation works with or without me.

Gap Handling

What about Comet Browser? ▼

Comet Browser doesn't have a documented public API, so the integration path depends on how your team actually uses it. There are three options, covered in detail on the Solution page: webhook callbacks (if supported), scheduled data exports, or browser automation via Playwright as a last resort.

The onboarding form asks specifically about your Comet Browser usage so I can scope the right approach before we start.

Can you manage LinkedIn Ads and Google Ads? ▼

I don't manage ad creative, audience targeting, or campaign strategy. That's a separate specialization.

What I do build is the automation around ad data: lead form sync (LinkedIn Ads lead gen forms -> CRM), campaign performance data feeding into the scoring model (leads from high-performing campaigns get a scoring boost), budget alerts, and conversion tracking that flows into the weekly digest. Your ads team keeps managing campaigns; the data flows into the pipeline automatically.

What about SEO tools (Ahrefs, SEMrush, Screaming Frog)? ▼

I can pull data from these tools into Make.com scenarios. Concrete example: a weekly Ahrefs ranking change alert that fires a Slack notification when a target keyword drops more than 5 positions. Or a monthly SEMrush backlink report that auto-populates a Google Sheet.

I don't do the SEO work itself (keyword research, content optimization, link building). But the data from these tools can feed into the pipeline. Organic visitors who convert through a tracked landing page can get a scoring boost in the lead scoring model.

Screaming Frog is trickier since it's a desktop app. If you run scheduled crawls, I can import the output files into Make.com. The onboarding form asks about this.

Can you integrate Google Analytics and Search Console? ▼

Yes. GA4 and Search Console both have APIs that Make.com can connect to. Use cases: pull conversion data into the scoring model (organic visitors who convert score higher), surface traffic anomalies in the weekly digest, or trigger alerts when a key landing page's conversion rate drops.

This is Phase 3 work. It adds value once the core pipeline is running and you want to enrich scoring with organic traffic signals.

What if a tool in our stack doesn't have an API? ▼

Three fallback paths, in order of preference:

  • CSV/data export: If the tool can export data files, Make.com can import them on a schedule. Most tools support this even without an API.
  • Email parsing: Some tools send notification emails. Make.com can monitor an inbox and parse those emails to extract data. Not elegant, but reliable.
  • Browser automation: As a last resort, Playwright can automate browser interactions to extract data. This is more fragile (breaks if the UI changes) and slower, so I only recommend it when there's no alternative.

Technical Questions

What happens if Make.com or n8n goes down? ▼

Both platforms have their own uptime guarantees (Make.com: 99.9% SLA on Business plan, n8n Cloud: similar). Outages are rare but happen.

The pipeline is designed so a temporary outage doesn't lose data. Webhook payloads that arrive during a Make.com outage get queued and processed when the service recovers. n8n has built-in retry logic for failed executions.

If an outage lasts long enough to matter, the error alerting system sends you a Slack notification so you know. And because the two platforms are independent, one being down doesn't take the other offline.

How do you handle data privacy (GDPR)? ▼

Both Make.com and n8n offer EU-hosted instances. Since you're based in Tallinn, we'd use EU data centers so lead data stays within the EU. Make.com's EU zone is eu1.make.com. n8n Cloud has an EU region option.

The pipeline processes contact data (names, emails, company info) which is personal data under GDPR. The key considerations: legitimate interest basis for B2B prospecting, data minimization (we only store what's needed for scoring and routing), and your existing privacy policy should cover automated processing. I can help configure data retention policies in the CRM and Make.com data stores if needed.

Can our team modify the automations ourselves? ▼

That's the whole point of using Make.com and n8n instead of custom code. Both are visual platforms where you can see every step of the workflow, trace data flow, and make changes by dragging modules around.

Every scenario and workflow comes with documentation explaining what it does and how to modify common settings. Scoring weights, sequence timing, and routing thresholds live in Google Sheets specifically so your team can adjust them without opening Make.com or n8n at all.

For structural changes (adding a new source, changing the scoring algorithm), that's what the retainer covers. But day-to-day configuration is designed to be self-service.

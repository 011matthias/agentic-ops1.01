Data Flow

How leads move through the automation pipeline, from first contact to qualified opportunity.

Pipeline Overview

Five stages, each with a defined input and output. Leads enter from multiple sources and exit as a routed action. Nothing falls through the gaps.

1
Capture
Webhooks and API listeners pull leads from LinkedIn, Apollo, HeyReach, web forms, and Reddit. Each source delivers via a dedicated Make.com webhook so failures on one channel don't affect the others.
2
Normalize
Different data formats get mapped to a single schema. An Apollo contact and a LinkedIn connection accept arrive with different field names and structures. This stage makes them identical before anything downstream touches them. Deduplication runs against existing records before passing forward.
3
Enrich
Apollo API adds company data, role, seniority, and firmographics. Fields that can't be enriched automatically get flagged for manual review rather than silently left blank. This keeps data quality measurable.
4
Score
n8n combines engagement signals with firmographic data to produce a lead score from 0 to 100. The scoring weights and routing thresholds are configurable without touching code.
5
Route
Hot leads trigger immediate notification. Warm leads enter a nurture sequence. Cool leads go into a weekly digest. Cold leads get archived with a quarterly re-evaluation flag.
Full observability

Every stage logs its input and output. If a lead gets stuck or misrouted, you can trace exactly where and why. Nothing is a black box.

Capture Stage

Each source connects differently. Here's how each one feeds into the pipeline.

LinkedIn via HeyReach webhook

HeyReach fires a webhook to Make.com on three events: connection accepted, message replied, and profile viewed (when viewing is part of a campaign sequence). Each event type carries different data, so the normalizer maps them to the common schema before anything downstream sees them.

Apollo prospecting lists

New contacts from prospecting list exports come in via the Apollo API. Make.com polls for new entries on a schedule, or Apollo can fire a webhook when a new contact hits a list threshold. Contact alerts for saved searches work the same way. Apollo is often the highest-volume source, so rate limit management is built into this leg of the pipeline.

Web forms

Form submissions post directly to a Make.com webhook endpoint. The payload maps cleanly to the schema because you control the form fields. These leads typically score higher on intent signals since the person came to you rather than being prospected.

Reddit keyword monitoring

n8n polls RSS feeds for subreddits and search terms relevant to your ICP. When a matching post appears, it gets captured as a lead candidate. These don't carry standard contact data, so they go through a different normalization path and typically require a manual outreach step before entering the main sequence.

Enrichment Stage

After normalization, every lead goes through Apollo for enrichment before scoring.

What Apollo adds

The enrichment call fills in fields that weren't present in the original source data: company name, company size, industry, revenue band, job title, seniority level, and LinkedIn URL. For contacts that came in with just a name and email, this turns a partial record into something you can actually score.

Handling missing data

Not every contact enriches cleanly. When Apollo can't match a contact, the record gets a needs_review flag rather than proceeding with blank fields. A daily digest lists flagged contacts so you can decide whether to manually research them or archive them. This keeps the scoring model accurate because it doesn't run on incomplete data.

Rate limit management

Apollo's API has request limits that vary by plan. The enrichment step in Make.com uses a queue with configurable pacing. If you're on a lower-tier Apollo plan, the queue throttles automatically rather than burning your limit in a single spike. The current setting is conservative by default and can be tuned upward once you know your usage pattern.

Scoring Stage

Scoring runs in n8n after enrichment completes. Two dimensions, each on a 0-50 scale. The combined score determines routing.

Engagement score (0-50)

Based on signals from the capture source. A LinkedIn connection accept is worth less than a reply to an outreach message, which is worth less than an inbound web form submission. HeyReach activity adds signal when a contact has opened multiple messages. The weights are editable in the n8n workflow without redeploying.

Firmographic score (0-50)

Based on how well the contact's company matches your ICP. Company size, industry, and seniority level each contribute points. A 200-person SaaS company with a VP-level contact in your target vertical scores near 50. A solo freelancer in an unrelated industry scores near 0.

Score routing table

Score Range Classification Action
80-100 Hot Lead Immediate Slack and email notification to sales
50-79 Warm Lead Enter 5-touch nurture sequence
20-49 Cool Lead Weekly digest and long-term drip
0-19 Cold Lead Archive, re-evaluate quarterly

The thresholds (80, 50, 20) are parameters in the n8n workflow. After a few weeks of data, you'll have a clear picture of where the natural breakpoints are for your specific audience. Adjusting them takes one edit, no redeployment required.

Routing Stage

After scoring, each lead gets a specific next action based on its classification.

Hot leads (80-100)

The pipeline fires a real-time Slack message and email to whoever handles sales follow-up. The notification includes the contact's name, company, score, source channel, and a direct link to their enriched profile in your CRM. A calendar booking link gets attached so the salesperson can send it immediately without composing anything. The CRM record is updated with the score and a priority flag at the same time.

Warm leads (50-79)

These enter a 5-touch automated email sequence built in n8n. Timing between touches is configurable. If a warm lead replies at any point, the sequence stops and it gets reclassified as hot. If the lead clicks a link in one of the emails, the engagement score gets recalculated and they may cross the hot threshold automatically.

Cool leads (20-49)

Cool leads get added to a weekly digest email and a lower-frequency drip sequence. They stay in the pipeline. If they engage at any point (email open with a click, form fill, return visit), the engagement score updates and they can move up.

Cold leads (0-19)

Cold leads get archived in the CRM with a quarterly re-evaluation date. n8n runs a weekly job that checks archived leads against a re-score criteria. If your ICP definition changes, you can re-score the entire archive without manual review.

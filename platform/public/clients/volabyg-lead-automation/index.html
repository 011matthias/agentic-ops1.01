Two Problems, One Root Cause

The spam problem and the count mismatch look like two separate issues. They usually share a root cause: warm leads are being sent through cold-outreach infrastructure, and the handoff between Facebook and the sequence was never tested end to end with real volume.

Instantly is a cold-outreach tool. It is built around rotating mailboxes, warmup, and aggressive sending to people who never asked to hear from you. Your leads are the opposite: they opted in through a form seconds earlier. Warm leads sent through cold infrastructure is one of the most common reasons legitimate mail lands in spam. The sender does not match what the receiving mail server expects, so it gets filtered.

The count mismatch is a transfer problem, not just a spam problem. If Facebook says 100 leads and your sequence only shows engagement consistent with 70, the missing 30 may never have reached Instantly at all. Lead Ads to Sheet to Instantly is three handoffs, and any one of them can silently drop rows: a webhook that times out, a sheet column that shifts, a sync that skips duplicates too aggressively. Spam hides some of the gap; lost leads hide the rest.

What a Public Lookup Already Shows

Before touching any of your accounts, a public DNS lookup of volabyg.dk tells most of the story. These records are published to the world; anyone can read them.

SPF  v=spf1 include:spf.simply.com -all
DMARC  v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc@robot.simply.com
MX  mx.simply.com

Your domain is locked down to Simply.com only, with a hard fail (-all) on any other sender and a DMARC policy of reject, the strictest setting. On your main mailbox, that is good security. For sending, it has a sharp consequence:

  • If Instantly sends as @volabyg.dk, every message fails SPF and DMARC tells the receiver to reject it. That mail does not reach the inbox at all.
  • If Instantly sends from a separate cold domain, your warm leads get email from a sender they do not recognize, which lowers trust and raises the spam rate.

Either way, the architecture is the cause, not bad luck. The audit confirms which path you are on and fixes it. We did not log into anything to learn this; it is the first two minutes of the audit, shown here so you can see how we work.

Meditation-Specific Flags

Meditation apps have compliance risks that generic GDPR checklists miss. The scanner includes Filip-specific checks for each of these categories.

Health Data Classification (GDPR Article 9)

If the app tracks meditation sessions, mood scores, sleep patterns, or breathing data, this may constitute "health data" under GDPR. Special category data requires explicit consent and a documented legal basis -- not just a checkbox at signup.

Push Notification Consent

UK requires opt-in consent under PECR. Canada requires express consent under CASL. Australia and New Zealand have different thresholds. Filip's privacy policy needs to address each market's rules separately -- one clause will not cover all five.

Cross-Border Data Transfers

If your servers are in the US but your users are in the EU, you need documented transfer mechanisms -- Standard Contractual Clauses or an adequacy decision. This is where most apps fail GDPR audits. The scanner flags missing transfer documentation by jurisdiction.

App Store Privacy Labels

Apple's App Tracking Transparency and Google's Data Safety section require accurate disclosure of data collection practices. These must align with your privacy policy. Inconsistencies between your policy and your app store labels are a common audit failure point.

Analytics SDK Data Sharing

If the app uses Firebase, Amplitude, Mixpanel, or similar SDKs, the privacy policy must disclose the data shared with these third parties. Each SDK has different data processing implications under GDPR. The scanner checks for generic versus specific SDK disclosure.

Children's Data

If the app does not gate for age, Filip may need to address children's data processing under Article 8 (EU GDPR), COPPA (if US users exist), and equivalent provisions in other jurisdictions. The scanner flags missing age verification or parental consent language.

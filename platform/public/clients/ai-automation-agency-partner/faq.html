Questions and Answers

The things you'd want to know before working with someone new.

What tools do you work with?

My primary platforms are Make.com and n8n for workflow orchestration. I write custom code in Python and JavaScript when the logic needs it. For AI, I integrate OpenAI and Claude APIs for classification, generation, and structured output. I also work with databases (MySQL, PostgreSQL), Google Sheets, Airtable, and most REST APIs.

How do you handle projects with unclear scope?

I start with a short discovery phase. Usually a 30-60 minute call plus a few async messages. I map the current workflow, identify what needs to change, and deliver a scope document before building anything. If the scope shifts during the build, I flag it early so there are no surprises.

What's your timezone and availability?

I'm based in Germany (CET). That's 6 hours ahead of US East Coast. There's solid overlap in the morning for real-time communication. Outside overlap hours, I work async. I typically respond within a few hours during business days. Currently available 20-30 hours per week.

Can you work directly with our clients?

Yes. I've worked directly with non-technical stakeholders and I'm comfortable running discovery calls, presenting solutions, and handling feedback. I can operate under your brand or as a named partner, whichever works for your model.

What happens if something breaks after delivery?

I build with monitoring and error handling from the start, so most issues surface before they affect end users. If something does break, ongoing optimization is included with every engagement. I don't charge extra for fixing things I built.

How do you handle multiple projects at once?

I focus on one project at a time within a work block. If you have multiple things running, I'll schedule them in priority order and give you clear ETAs. I won't context-switch mid-task and deliver half-finished work.

What does your handoff look like?

Every delivery comes with a Loom walkthrough and written documentation. I'll walk through the system, explain the design decisions, and show how to maintain or extend it. If your team needs to make changes later, they'll have the context to do it.

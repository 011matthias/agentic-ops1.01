Consent Flows

Every data collection point in the patient journey records explicit consent before any personal data is stored or processed. Consent timestamps and source are logged in Zoho CRM for audit trail purposes.

Consent touchpoints

  • Screening form — checkbox consent with timestamp recorded before submission is accepted
  • Booking form — separate consent field for appointment data processing, distinct from screening consent
  • Email opt-in — double opt-in flow via Brevo; marketing emails only sent after confirmation link is clicked
  • SMS opt-in — explicit consent captured at intake; recorded in CRM before any SMS is sent
  • Intercom chat — consent banner displayed before the chat widget collects any personal data
Audit trail

All consent records — including the source form, timestamp, and consent text version — are stored as structured fields in Zoho CRM. This gives you a queryable audit trail if consent is ever challenged.

Consent withdrawal

Withdrawal of consent is handled per channel. Email unsubscribes are processed by Brevo and synced to the CRM. SMS opt-out keywords are handled by MessageBird. A patient record in the CRM reflects the current consent status across all channels at any time.

Data Processing Agreements

A Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is required from every tool vendor before that tool is used to process personal data. The roles within the system are clearly defined.

Data Controller
Menovia
Determines the purpose and means of processing patient personal data. Responsible for the lawful basis and consent management.
Data Processors
Tool vendors
Zoho, Brevo, MessageBird, Cal.com, Intercom, Typeform, Mollie — each processes data on behalf of Menovia under a DPA.
Sub-processor
n8n
Processes data in transit between systems as an automation layer. Acts as a sub-processor to Menovia with its own DPA and EU data residency.

DPA checklist

  • Zoho CRM — DPA available, covers EU data centre selection
  • n8n Cloud — DPA available, covers sub-processor obligations
  • Mollie — DPA available, PCI DSS compliant for payment data
  • Brevo — DPA available, covers email marketing processing
  • MessageBird — DPA available, covers SMS communication data
  • Cal.com — DPA available, covers scheduling and booking data
  • Intercom — DPA available, covers chat and support data
  • Typeform — DPA available, covers form submission data

A complete DPA checklist document, with links to each vendor's current DPA and instructions for countersigning where required, is provided as part of the project handover package.

Retention Policy

Data retention is defined per data category, aligned with Dutch legal requirements and GDPR's storage limitation principle. Retaining data longer than necessary is treated as a compliance risk, not a convenience.

Medical Data Separation

Critical separation principle

Medical data never enters the automation system. Medical records stay exclusively in your NEN 7510-certified Electronic Health Record system. This separation is fundamental to the architecture, not an afterthought.

The automation system operates exclusively on operational data: contact information, appointment scheduling, payment status, and communication preferences. None of these data categories constitute medical data under Dutch or EU law.

What the automation system handles

  • Patient name, email address, phone number
  • Appointment date, time, and status
  • Payment confirmation (reference number only — no card data)
  • Communication preferences and consent flags
  • Screening form responses (eligibility questions, not clinical assessments)

What stays exclusively in the EHR

  • Clinical diagnoses and treatment records
  • Prescription history
  • Lab results and test outcomes
  • Physician consultation notes
  • Any data that constitutes a special category under GDPR Article 9
NEN 7510 boundary

The handoff point between the automation system and the EHR is a notification only: "Patient X has booked an appointment." No clinical data crosses this boundary in either direction. The physician retrieves clinical context from the EHR directly, as they do today.

This architecture means the automation system never processes special category data under GDPR Article 9. The legal basis for processing operational data is straightforward contract performance and legitimate interest, with no requirement for explicit medical consent for the automation layer itself.

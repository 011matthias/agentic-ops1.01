Centerpiece

The architecture

How the bot stays fast and friendly without ever being wrong about a shipment.

The core split: judgment vs facts

Every message runs through two layers that never mix responsibilities.

The judgment layer is a small language model call. It reads a German customer message and answers three narrow questions: which order is this about, what is the customer asking (a status request, a correction, or something else), and how should the reply read so it sounds like your shop and not like a robot. That is all it does.

The fact layer is plain Python. It calls your ERP interface, gets the tracking link, the tracking number, the carrier, and the status, and drops those values into the reply exactly as the ERP returned them. The model is never asked to remember or generate a tracking number. If the ERP returns nothing usable, the fact layer stops the auto-reply and routes the message to a person with everything already gathered.

Why this matters

The expensive failure for a shipment bot is a confident wrong answer: a tracking number that does not exist, a carrier that is not the real one. Keeping every fact on the deterministic side removes that failure mode by construction, not by prompt-tuning and hoping.

The pipeline, end to end

1, Ingest
One message, any channel
All-Inkl email arrives over standard IMAP. Amazon Message Center arrives through the Amazon Selling Partner API. Both are normalized into one internal message shape so the rest of the pipeline does not care where it came from.
↓
2, Understand
Extract order and intent
One small LLM call pulls the order reference, the customer identity hints, and the intent label from a fixed set: shipment status, tracking correction, or out of scope. It returns a confidence score with every field.
↓
3, Look up
Deterministic ERP query
Python calls your ERP interface with the order reference. It gets the tracking link, tracking number, carrier, and status back. No model involved. Handles not found, multiple matches, and slow or failing ERP responses explicitly.
↓
4, Compose
Grounded German reply
A German template carries the structure and the Sie-form politeness. The model only adjusts wording for the specific case. The tracking facts are inserted verbatim from step 3 and are never passed through the model as free text.
↓
5, Decide to send
Confidence gate and channel rules
High confidence and a clean ERP match: the reply sends automatically on the original channel. Low confidence, no match, or out of scope: it becomes a drafted reply in a human queue with full context. The Amazon channel additionally passes a policy filter before anything leaves.
↓
6, Log
Every decision recorded
Message, detected intent, confidence, the exact ERP data used, the action taken, and the channel. This is what makes the bot debuggable and what makes a long-term partnership realistic instead of a black box.

The two intents in detail

"Where is my shipment?"

The most common ticket and the cleanest to automate. The bot identifies the order, pulls the live tracking data from the ERP, and replies in German with the carrier, the tracking number, the tracking link, and the current status. If the order has not shipped yet, it says so honestly with the expected timing if the ERP exposes it, rather than inventing a date.

Correction and update requests on tracking

These are the follow-ups: "the tracking link does not work", "this is not my parcel", "the address is wrong". The bot classifies the correction type and does one of two things. If your ERP exposes a safe write path for that correction, it confirms the change with the customer and applies it. If it does not, or the request is ambiguous, it acknowledges the customer in German and routes a structured handoff to your team, so nothing silently fails. Which corrections are safe to automate depends on what your ERP API allows, and that is the first thing I would confirm with you on the Onboarding page.

Why German is a design constraint, not a setting

Exclusively German is not a translation toggle. It shapes the templates (formal Sie by default), the tone, the date and address formatting, and the fallback behavior when a customer writes in another language. The model is instructed and constrained to German, the templates are written in German by default, and the test set is German tickets. Encoding is handled end to end so umlauts and the sharp s never arrive garbled through IMAP or the Amazon API.

Stack, in one place

  • Orchestration: n8n. The email trigger, the Amazon poll, the branching, and the human-handoff routing live here, visible and editable, which suits a long-term partnership.
  • Logic and integrations: Python for the ERP client, the Amazon Selling Partner API client, normalization, and the audit log.
  • Model: a small model in the GPT-4o-mini or Claude Haiku class, used only for extraction and German phrasing, swappable through one config value. Reasoning is on the Investment page.
  • State: PostgreSQL for thread state, the handoff queue, and the audit log.

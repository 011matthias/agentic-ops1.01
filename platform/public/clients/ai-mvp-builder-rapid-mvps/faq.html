Buy vs build

What if Algolia or Pinecone solves this at a fraction of the cost?

Then I would tell you to use Algolia or Pinecone. The job post is explicit: "if a cheap SaaS tool or existing API solves the problem better than custom code, we expect you to say so." I do. The reason custom wins on messy data is that the cleanup and confidence-scoring need to happen before search; SaaS search assumes you have already cleaned the data. If your supplier feeds are cleaner than I am assuming, SaaS is the right answer.

How do I know which path fits before committing?

That is exactly what the paid test is for. 10 hours, $400, real data, real matching quality on day one. After Phase 0 you will have working code on a slice of your products and a written report on what the matching quality looks like. That is enough to make the SaaS-vs-custom call with actual numbers.

Accuracy and quality

What matching accuracy can I expect?

Honest answer: I will not quote a percentage until I see your data. Matching accuracy depends on how clean your supplier feeds are, how many brand and category overlaps there are, and how consistent the specs are. After Phase 0 I will have real numbers on a slice of your products and can give you a realistic range.

How does the team know whether to trust a match?

Every match returns a confidence score (cosine similarity rescaled to 0 to 1) visible in the UI. High confidence (around 0.85 and above) usually means "trust it," mid range (roughly 0.65 to 0.85) means "double-check," below that means "probably not a match." The exact thresholds get calibrated against the team's own feedback after a week or two of real use.

What happens when the model gets it wrong?

The UI has a "this match is wrong" button. Clicks write to a corrections table I use to tune ranking and (optionally) fine-tune a re-ranker. Errors are inputs, not embarrassments. The feedback loop is the thing that makes this work over time.

Costs and operations

How much does the OpenAI API actually cost?

For embeddings at 2,000 products with text-embedding-3-small, the initial embed costs a small one-time amount, usually under $1 at this volume. Re-embedding only happens when rows change, so ongoing cost is minimal. The expensive AI calls would be if we add LLM-based reranking or spec-filling later; those would be itemized before they are added.

What if the data refresh needs to be automated?

That is part of Phase 1. The pipeline (normalize, dedupe, re-embed only on change) can run scheduled (cron or n8n) or on-demand. We pick which based on how often your suppliers update their feeds.

Handoff and ownership

What does "handoff" actually include?

A written document covering: how each script works, where the code lives, how to swap models, how to add a new supplier feed, how to interpret the test suite results, and a list of the design decisions and tradeoffs (so future-you knows why something is the way it is). Plus a walkthrough session if you want one. Goal: you can hire any competent Python developer to maintain or extend this. You are not dependent on me.

Who owns the code?

You do. All code, tests, docs, and the corrections data. Nothing I deliver depends on a proprietary library or service that you would have to license from me.

Scope and changes

What if we want to add new product categories or suppliers later?

The pipeline is built to be extended. New supplier feeds plug into the same normalize function with a small adapter. New categories add to the category filter rules. The architecture is deliberately not clever about it; clever architectures break when requirements change.

What if our requirements change during the build?

Standard answer: small changes get absorbed, larger ones go through a scope conversation before they are built. The phase structure (paid test, then Phase 1, then Phase 2) gives natural points to recheck scope without breaking momentum. The posting mentioned not needing detailed specs to start; that works fine here because each phase has a defined output to anchor on.

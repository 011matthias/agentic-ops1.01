Common Questions

Answers to questions about the course build process, tools, and how we'll work together.

Course Design

Won't this end up as a generic AI tutorial?

No. Every module is built around a complete system, not isolated tool demos. Students won't just learn "how to prompt ChatGPT." They'll learn how content moves from an idea through AI drafting to publishing, with automation handling the repeatable parts.

The systems are designed to be reusable across different businesses. A student in beauty and wellness uses the same content flow as someone in coaching or e-commerce. The underlying logic transfers.

How do you simplify complex automation concepts for non-technical users?

By focusing on the "what" and "why" before the "how." Each system starts with a simple diagram showing where data comes from, what happens to it, and where it goes. The actual tool setup (Make.com, Zapier) is presented as visual step-by-step walkthroughs, not technical documentation.

I've built 50+ Make.com scenarios and the biggest lesson I've taken from that work: if someone needs to read instructions to use it, it's too complex. That standard applies to everything in this course.

What does "clean, modern slides" mean in practice?

Visual-first. One idea per slide. No walls of text or bullet-point overload. Diagrams and annotated screenshots where possible. Think Apple keynote style, not corporate training deck.

You'll see samples before approving Milestone 3. If the style doesn't match your vision, we adjust before anything is finalized.

Tools and Tech

Which AI tools will be part of the course?

ChatGPT as the primary AI layer. It's what Dae's students will already know, and it's the most documented tool for non-technical users. Make.com and Zapier for automation. Both are visual, no-code, and well-documented. AI avatar tools as a component where relevant to the content plan.

The exact tool mapping gets confirmed in Milestone 1 once we've aligned on which modules to include.

Can students actually import and use the workflow blueprints?

Yes. Make.com and Zapier both support blueprint and template imports. Students connect their own accounts, enter their API keys, and the workflow runs. The downloadable blueprint on the Overview page is a working example of this format.

Every blueprint in the course will be annotated so students understand what each step does before they run it.

What if a student doesn't have a Make.com or Zapier account?

Both tools have free tiers, and the workflows are designed to stay within free-tier limits. Students won't need to pay anything extra to follow along with the course material.

I'll include a setup guide for both tools as part of the course materials so new users don't hit any friction getting started.

Working Together

What do you need from me to get started?

Brand guidelines if you have them, your target audience profile, examples of the content topics you'd want the course to cover, and access to any tools you're already using. The Onboarding page collects all of this in one place.

Nothing needs to be finalized. Rough notes work fine. We'll refine everything together in Milestone 1.

How do we communicate during the project?

Upwork messaging for day-to-day updates. Each milestone ends with a Loom walkthrough video so Dae can review every deliverable at her own pace before we move to the next stage. No live meeting required unless you'd prefer it.

What if I want to change direction mid-project?

That's what the milestone structure is for. Each milestone ends with a review checkpoint. If you want to shift the focus, we adjust the next milestone's scope together before starting it. There are no surprises mid-build because nothing moves forward without your sign-off.

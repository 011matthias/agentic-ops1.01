Experience and Fit

You don't have direct GHL or Retell AI experience. How does that work?

The integration patterns are the same across platforms: webhooks trigger workflows, API calls pass data between systems, IF nodes filter event types, field mappings transform payloads. GHL has a native n8n node, and Retell's webhook system sends structured JSON callbacks. That's the same pattern I work with in every client environment I run.

What I don't have is time spent in the GHL or Retell UI specifically. That's a UI familiarity gap, not an architecture gap. Your SOPs and Loom walkthroughs are exactly how I close that. The test setup in Week 1 is designed to prove this out on a real client environment before any ongoing commitment is made.

Can you work from our existing SOPs and Loom recordings?

Yes. That's my preferred onboarding method. I'll go through the SOPs in full, follow the Loom walkthroughs step by step, and take notes on anything that isn't covered or that I'd do differently. If there's a gap, I'll flag it with a proposed solution rather than guessing.

I won't ask you to explain something that's already in a SOP. If it's documented, I'll read it. Questions I do ask will be specific: a field name that's ambiguous, a step that has two interpretations, a credential I need access to.

What happens if this doesn't work out?

The test setup in Week 1 is specifically designed for this scenario. You evaluate my output directly: did I follow the SOP correctly, did the end-to-end call flow work, is the documentation clear, was communication prompt and specific. If the answer to any of those is no, you haven't spent anything beyond a single setup fee.

That setup also leaves you with a fully documented client environment regardless of outcome. The webhook URLs, workflow IDs, and credential references are all written down. If you decide not to continue, you have a usable artifact, not just a sunk cost.

There's no ongoing obligation until you decide the trial met your bar.

Communication and Availability

I'm in Karlsruhe, Germany (CET/CEST), which is 7 hours ahead of Austin. In practice, this works well for async: requests you send at the end of your business day are on my desk first thing in my morning. You often have completed work waiting when you start your day.

I communicate via written updates. If I hit a blocker, I flag it immediately rather than waiting for a scheduled check-in. I don't need meetings to raise issues or confirm decisions. If a quick call is useful, I'm available in the overlap window (your late morning, my early evening).

How do you handle urgent issues?

For production-critical issues (broken webhook, failed call flow, Retell agent not triggering), I respond within the same business day. My working hours cover your morning, so most issues raised at your start of day get a response before your afternoon.

Structured error handling and logging in n8n means most failures are diagnosable from the execution log without needing live access to the client account. I can usually identify root cause, propose a fix, and push it within a few hours of the report coming in.

Working Together

What does a typical change request look like?

Most requests fall into one of these categories:

  • Updating a Retell agent's system prompt to handle a new edge case or objection
  • Adding a new data field to an n8n workflow (capturing a new GHL custom field, passing it to Supabase)
  • Modifying a GHL sequence trigger (changing the pipeline stage that kicks off the voice call)
  • Debugging a webhook that stopped firing after a GHL platform update
  • Adjusting call_analyzed event filtering logic when a new outcome type appears

Most of these take 30-90 minutes. I document every change with what was modified, why, and what to watch for. That keeps your team informed even when I'm the one making the edits.

How do you document changes?

Every change gets a written update: what was modified, why, and what to watch for. For workflow changes, that includes the n8n workflow name, the specific nodes edited, and the expected behavior after the change. For prompt refinements, it includes the edge case that triggered the change and how the updated prompt handles it.

Setup documentation is more detailed: webhook URLs, workflow IDs, credential references, Retell agent IDs, and the GHL sub-account configuration. This means if I'm unavailable for any reason, someone else can pick up where I left off without reverse-engineering anything.

What does a typical weekly update look like?

A written summary at the end of each week covering: changes completed (with per-change detail), any issues caught proactively, pending items waiting on input from your side, and observations about patterns that could be improved. The format stays consistent so you can scan it quickly without reading a wall of text.

If something needs your attention mid-week (a blocker, a scope question, a credential expiry), I flag it immediately rather than batching it into the weekly update. The weekly summary is for context, not for surfacing urgent items.

Why $30/hr on a $15-25/hr listing

Your listing is clear about the budget range. I want to be equally clear about the rate, rather than dancing around it.

Three reasons for the gap

First: Germany-based. I'm in the same timezone as your office in Grobenzell, which means real-time coordination during business hours, no async delays on urgent fixes. Second: direct experience with Hero Software and LexOffice. Most Make.com specialists have never touched these systems; I have. That's weeks of ramp-up time I'm not billing for. Third: migration carries more risk than a fresh build. Preserving your existing logic, coordinating webhook cutovers, and avoiding data loss requires care that greenfield work doesn't.

The rate isn't a negotiating position. It reflects what this project actually requires. That said, the hours aren't fixed. Once the migration is complete and the scenarios are stable, we can dial back to whatever your actual maintenance needs are, which is often far less than 20 hours per week.

How the hours adjust over time

Migration phase: higher intensity, roughly 20 hrs/week for 4-5 weeks. Stabilization phase: monitoring and fixes, around 5-10 hrs/week. Ongoing maintenance: change requests and optimizations, billed at actual hours with no retainer requirement unless you prefer one.

After the migration is done

Once all scenarios are live in the new account and the old account is decommissioned, the engagement shifts. You're not locked into a retainer.

  • Change requests: Billed at $30/hr, scoped before work starts. Small changes (a field mapping, a filter condition) are usually under 1 hour.
  • New scenario builds: Same rate. New automations follow the same methodology: design, test, document, monitor.
  • Optimization: If a scenario is running inefficiently (too many operations, slow execution), I'll flag it and fix it as part of ongoing maintenance.
  • No minimum hours: Some months will be quiet. You pay for what's actually done.
The goal is stability, not dependency

The documentation delivered at the end of migration is yours. If you ever want to bring this in-house or hand it to another contractor, you'll have everything they need to pick it up without starting from scratch.

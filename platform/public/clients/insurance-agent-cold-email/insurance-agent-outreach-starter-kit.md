# Insurance Agent Cold Outreach Starter Kit

Prepared by UnpauseAI. A working starter kit for cold outbound to insurance agents: where to source them, how to keep email out of spam, and a sequence you can send. This is the same playbook we run as a managed service; you can use this version yourself or hand the whole motion to us.

## 1. Where to source insurance agents

Insurance agents are one of the most sourceable B2B audiences, because licensed agents are public record.

- **State Department of Insurance (DOI) registries.** Most US states publish a license lookup. Licensed agents and agencies appear with name, license type, and often agency affiliation. Public, free, authoritative.
- **NIPR (National Insurance Producer Registry).** Producer data across states.
- **Apollo.io.** Filter by title and industry (see the recipe below) for emails plus firmographics in one place.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator.** Title and industry filters; strong for independent agency owners and producers.
- **Agency directories and associations.** NAIFA member directories, local independent-agent association lists, carrier "find an agent" tools.

### Apollo filter recipe (starting point)

- Titles: `insurance agent`, `insurance broker`, `producer`, `agency owner`, `agency principal`, `account manager` (insurance)
- Industry: `Insurance`
- Company headcount: `1-50` (independent agencies convert faster than carrier employees)
- Geography: your target states
- Email status: `verified` only

Always run the final list through a separate verification pass before sending. The single biggest cause of spam-foldering is a list with dead addresses driving bounces up.

## 2. Deliverability checklist (keep it out of spam)

Run every box before the first cold send.

- [ ] Buy **separate sending domains**, not your primary domain (e.g. a `.co` or `getX.com` variant). Your main domain is never put at risk.
- [ ] Set up **SPF, DKIM, and DMARC** DNS records on every sending domain. These prove the domain is allowed to send mail.
- [ ] Create a small number of mailboxes per domain (2-3). More domains, not more mailboxes per domain.
- [ ] **Warm up** every mailbox for 2-3 weeks before any cold email. New inboxes that blast cold volume get flagged immediately.
- [ ] Cap volume at roughly 30-40 cold emails per mailbox per day. Scale by adding mailboxes, not by sending more per box.
- [ ] Verify the list (bounce target under 2 percent).
- [ ] Plain-text style. No images, no link-heavy footers, minimal links in the first email.
- [ ] One clear opt-out and a physical mailing address (CAN-SPAM).
- [ ] Reply detection that stops the sequence the moment someone responds.

## 3. A starter sequence for insurance agents

Four touches over about two weeks. Keep each email short, specific, and easy to reply to. Replace the bracketed parts with your real product detail.

**Email 1 (Day 1): the problem, not the product**

> Subject: quoting time at [agency name]?
>
> Hi [First name], quick one. Most agents I talk to lose hours a week reading policies line by line before they can advise a client or quote a competitor's coverage. Is that roughly how it works for you, or have you already solved it?

**Email 2 (Day 3): what it does**

> Subject: re: quoting time
>
> Following up with the why. [Product] reads a policy and flags gaps, overlaps, and upsell openings in seconds, so you spend the time selling instead of reading. Worth a 10-minute look?

**Email 3 (Day 7): proof or specificity**

> Subject: 10 minutes this week?
>
> Not trying to be a pest. If it helps, I can show you exactly what it does with one of your own policy types on a quick screen share. No prep needed on your end. Open Thursday or Friday?

**Email 4 (Day 12): the break-up**

> Subject: closing the loop
>
> I will stop here so I am not cluttering your inbox. If selling more off the policies you already handle is ever a priority, just reply and I will send a 2-minute demo. Either way, good luck this quarter.

## 4. The one thing that decides it

The campaign mechanics matter, but the audience and the inbox reputation decide whether anyone ever reads the copy. Get a verified, well-targeted list and a properly warmed, authenticated sending setup, and an average sequence performs. Skip those and the best copy in the world lands in spam.

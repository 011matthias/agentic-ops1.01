Weeks 1 to 2: setup

Everything that makes the first campaign land happens here. The warm-up runs in the background through both weeks, which is why the first send sits at the end of week two and not before.

PhaseWhenWhat happens
List buildDays 1 to 4Source insurance agents from license registries, Apollo, Sales Navigator, and directories; verify every address to keep bounce under 2 percent.
Domains and mailboxesDays 1 to 3Buy the separate sending domains, create the mailboxes, and set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication.
Warm-upDay 1 onwardMailboxes start the 2 to 3 week warm-up immediately, building a normal sending history before cold volume.
First sequence writtenDays 4 to 8Write the short cold sequence for insurance agents and your product, ready to launch once inboxes are warm.

End of week 2: first send

As the warm-up completes, the first campaign goes out. Volume starts low and ramps as the inboxes keep maturing, so the early sends stay gentle and deliverability holds. From here, replies start coming in and the booking part of the loop kicks off.

Ongoing: managed monthly

After the first send, the work becomes a recurring cycle: send, handle replies, book demos onto your calendar, and report and tune every week. Lists get replenished so volume never dries up, and copy and targeting get adjusted based on what the numbers show. You stay month to month the whole way.

Why the first two weeks aren't faster

Mailboxes need 2 to 3 weeks of warm-up before they can carry cold volume without hurting deliverability. We start that on day one so the wait runs in parallel with the list and copy work, not after it.

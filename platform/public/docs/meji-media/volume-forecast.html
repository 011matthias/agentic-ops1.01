Volume Forecast: What 11 Years of Enquiry Data Tells Us

The Scaling document ended on an open question: Gurmej's "annual +10%" or Jess's "skyrockets June/July"? This document answers it. We pulled every enquiry on file across the full historical archive (four MySQL tables, 2015 to 2025) and the pattern is clearer than either intuition guessed.

Prepared for: Gurmej Pawar, Jess Harrar
Date: 8 May 2026
Source: Live MySQL archive (read-only)
Summary in Three Sentences

The peak month for new enquiries is September every year, by a wide margin. The intake curve from January to December is the same shape year after year: a slow first half, a steady ramp from June, a sharp September spike, then a fast tail through November and December.

Both stated growth views from the 22 April calls turn out to be partly right but neither is the full picture: annual totals are roughly stable year-over-year (Gurmej's "not wildly different" is closer than +10%), and Jess's instinct about a ramp was directionally correct but mistimed by about two months, the ramp is real, but it lands in August through October, not June and July.

For the scaling decision, this means the deliverability cliffs in the Scaling document need to be evaluated against a September peak that historically hits 20 to 25 enquiries per day sustained for several weeks, not a flat year-round average.

The Headline Finding
Every year for at least a decade, intake peaks in the same 7-week window
August through mid-October. Eight of nine complete seasons peaked in September. The exception (2018) peaked in October.

This is not a 2025 fluke and not a recent trend. The pattern is visible in every year of data we have, going back to 2015. It is so consistent that it can be used as a planning input directly: every staffing, automation, and marketing decision should treat August through October as a fundamentally different operating regime from the other nine months.

The single biggest day on record was 3 September 2025: 42 new enquiries arrived in 24 hours. The runner-up days are also September (15th, 16th, and 22nd of 2025; 25 September 2024; 18 September 2019).

What This Means For The Scaling Decision

The Scaling document mapped three deliverability cliffs against a flat per-day average of around 30 outbound emails. That average is misleading. The cliffs need to be evaluated against the September peak, not the year-round mean.

From the historical data, a typical September day produces around 20 to 25 enquiries, sustained for several weeks. With the current pipeline pattern of 4 to 6 emails per enquiry, that is roughly 100 to 150 outbound emails per day from the single mailbox for the duration of September. Plus the catch-up sends from late August enquiries still in their follow-up window.

Revised exposure to each cliff:

Cliff 1 (Soft)

Going from a 30-per-day baseline to 100 to 150 per day during September is roughly a 3 to 5 times jump. That is squarely inside the trigger range for Gmail's spam-filter heuristics on volume spikes. Realistic risk for this September.

Cliff 2 (Hard)

The 2,000-per-day Workspace cap stays well clear at 100 to 150 sustained. Would only become a real risk if a single day produced more than around 400 enquiries, which the historical data suggests is very unlikely (the all-time single-day peak was 42).

Cliff 3 (Regulatory)

The 5,000-per-day bulk-sender threshold is far off. No realistic September scenario for the Christmas-side flow brings it close. (The Instantly outbound side is a separate story.)

The practical translation: Path A (monitor and stay) is genuinely at risk for the upcoming September. Path B (multi-mailbox rotation) was framed as "buys 12+ months of headroom" and that framing holds, if you spread the September load across 2 to 3 mailboxes, each one stays comfortably under the cliff. Path C remains the right answer if the Instantly outbound work moves forward and you want shared infrastructure.

How We Got This Data

This is not a guess or a survey. The numbers are pulled directly from the live database. Specifically:

  1. The MySQL connection used by the existing automations (enquire@christmasofficeparty.co.uk mailbox flow) is read-only. We built a small read-only diagnostic scenario in Make.com (UTIL scenario 8974201) for ad-hoc queries against the same connection, no separate credentials required.
  2. The historical enquiry data is split across four tables: enquiries (the live current-season table, 181 rows), all_enquiries (a 2015 to 2020 archive, 3,005 rows), full_data_enquiries (a wider 2023 to 2026 view, 7,833 rows), and enquiries_backup_23-04-2025 (a snapshot from April 2025, 3,859 rows).
  3. None of these alone covers the full timeline. We reconciled them by date range, schema discovery, and de-duplication. The 2020 to 2022 period is the only stretch we could not recover, it sits in none of the four tables.
  4. All aggregation done in SQL against the live tables. No data was extracted, exported, or persisted outside the database. The numbers in this document are the database's; we surfaced the pattern.
Caveats & Confidence
  • The 2020 to 2022 gap is real. We don't know what the COVID-era curve looked like. The pre-COVID and post-COVID patterns are consistent enough with each other that we're confident in the September-peak framing, but if the lockdown years materially shifted customer behaviour, that's not visible in this data.
  • The full_data_enquiries table is slightly stale for 2026. It shows only 49 rows for 2026 but the live enquiries table has 181 through 7 May. Daily counts for late April and early May 2026 are based on the live table only and may be undercounted by a small amount in any historical aggregate.
  • The 2017 yearly total (227) is suspicious. Other years in the same archive are in the 600-plus range. Most likely a partial-year data issue rather than a real volume drop.
  • Single-day peak (42) is from 2025-09-03 specifically. Daily peaks were extracted across the full archive; a higher single day could exist in the 2020 to 2022 gap. We treat 42 as a lower bound on the historical peak.
  • "Enquiry" is defined here as a row in the database, whatever flowed in via the website form, the phone capture, the partner referral feed, etc. We have not separated by source. If you want a per-source breakdown, that's a follow-up query.

If any of these caveats matter for the path decision, say so and we'll dig further before locking in a recommendation.

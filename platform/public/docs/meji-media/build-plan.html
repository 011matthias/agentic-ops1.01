Your Outreach Build, in Plain Terms

The finished shape of what is being built for Meji, so you can see exactly what you are getting and how it runs week to week. Seven pieces, two non-negotiable rules, one operator accountable for all of it.

What This Is

This is the outreach engine rebuilt properly: the warm databases reactivated, cold data sourced and filtered before anything sends, every campaign on a real follow-up sequence, and a weekly report that never slips. It maps one to one onto the priorities you set out.

The point of writing it down like this is so nothing about the build is a black box. You can see each piece, what finished looks like, and who is on the hook for it.

Seven pieces. Two rules that never bend. One person who owns the outcome.
Christmas warm, Banter warm, cold-data sourcing, per-city cold lists, sample approval, follow-up sequences, the Monday report.
1 & 2. The Warm Campaigns

Your existing audiences are the strongest you have. They get rebuilt first because the calendar demands it, and they get treated with more care than a single blast.

Piece 1

Christmas warm campaign

Your Christmas list, cleaned and split by how each person actually relates to Meji. People with real history get familiar-tone emails. The rest get awareness-building emails that earn recognition before the season instead of assuming it. Three touches over about ten days, and it stops the moment someone replies.

Done looks like: live and sending ahead of the September rush, not competing with it.
Piece 2

Banter warm campaign

The same approach for Banter: lower volume, a quieter tone, on Banter's own mailboxes. Built once Christmas has proven the pattern once, so the second run is the proven playbook rather than a fresh experiment.

Done looks like: live on the Banter mailboxes, ramped at a calm pace.
3 & 4. Cold Data, Done Right This Time

The cold side failed before because the data was wrong, not because the system was wrong. So the data gets fixed properly, and you see it before it sends.

Piece 3

Cold-data provider decision

Apollo and its main competitors tested on the identical brief. A real sample pulled from each, scored on the same criteria. You get one page: the ranking, the samples, and the one I recommend. You pick from what you can see, not from sales claims.

Done looks like: a one-page comparison in your hands, decision yours.
Piece 4

Clean Christmas cold lists per city

For Birmingham, Leicester and Wolverhampton, a filtered list each: the right titles, the right company size, within range of the venue, deduped against the warm audience so no one is double-contacted.

Done looks like: three approved city lists live, each seen by you first.
5 & 6. The Two Rules That Never Bend

These two exist specifically because of what went wrong before. They are not negotiable down, by either of us. They are the reason the cold side does not bounce-pause again.

Piece 5

Sample approval before any cold send

A standing rule, not a one-off. Before any cold campaign goes out you see a sample of the real contacts, roughly 100 to 200, and you flag anything off. Nothing sends until you have said yes. This is the direct fix for what bounce-paused the old campaigns.

Done looks like: a logged yes from you on file before every cold send, every time.
Piece 6

A real follow-up sequence on every campaign

No single sends, ever, warm or cold. Three touches, the sequence stops the moment someone replies, and bounced addresses drop out on their own so they cannot drag a campaign down.

Done looks like: every live campaign on the 3-touch sequence with reply-stop verified.
7. The Monday Report

The six numbers you asked for, every Monday morning, in the same format every week so it is a thirty-second read: emails sent, bounce rate, replies, positive replies, opportunities and calls booked, and anything that needs your attention.

It is pulled straight from the system rather than typed up by hand, so it is accurate and it lands on time, every week, including the quiet ones. A quiet week gets an honest quiet report rather than silence. Consistency is the product here, and that is exactly what you get.

Why it stays simple

It is deliberately the six things you named and nothing bolted on. A report you can read in thirty seconds and trust every week is worth more than a longer one you have to decode.

Why September Drives the Calendar

This is not a guess about timing. We pulled eleven years of your own enquiry data. Every year, intake peaks hard in September, with August through October running far above the rest of the year. The detail is in the Volume Forecast.

What it means for the build: the warm rebuild has to be engaging people before the September peak, not introducing Meji to them during it. Cold campaigns ramp ahead of August or hold through September so they do not strain deliverability when your own enquiry inbox needs the headroom most. The order falls out of the data: warm and the cold-data evaluation start now, the rest follows.

How This Is Run

The build matters less than how it is run after it is built. This is set up as a managed system, not a stack of one-off tasks:

  • One operator owns the outcome. Matthias runs the build and the week-to-week operation. You always know who is accountable, and you hear from the same person every Monday.
  • The standards do not flex under pressure. The sample-approval gate and the follow-up-sequence rule hold even when it would be faster to skip them. That is the point of them.
  • The numbers are pulled from the live system, not hand-assembled, so what you read on Monday is what actually happened, not a tidied version of it.
  • You only get pulled in when it is genuinely your call: cold-list approval, provider choice, copy sign-off. Everything else is run and reported, not escalated.

That is the build. Pieces one and three start now in parallel, and the rest hangs off those two.

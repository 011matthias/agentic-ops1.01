The Goal

In your words: 5 to 10 big corporate clients that do multiple events through the year. That reframes success from counting bookings to winning accounts that come back. Polestar rebooking year on year is the model for it.

The money follows from your own figures: a typical contract runs £2,000 to £15,000 per event, and a multi-event client is £20,000 or more across a year. A handful of those accounts is a six-figure annual line, which is why the build favours a small number of high-value, repeat-buying accounts over raw volume.

September into the Christmas season is the window we both set as the point to judge the whole engagement on.

Three Routes to That Goal
1

Warm re-engagement

The proven converter. Your past attendees already know you, so this is where replies and bookings actually land; Four Car Audio is the most recent, revived by an automated follow-up.

Its one limit is that it sends from a single mailbox. A second warm sender before the season would let the best-converting campaign reach more people, and remove the single point of failure at the same time.

Live, converting
2

September corporate

The volume route, aimed at the £2k to £15k per-event contracts. Corporate buying is seasonal and switches on in the autumn, so this is timed for a 1 September start.

The copy you approved is locked, and the UK-wide target list is in build now so it is ready when the window opens.

In build for September
3

Referral partners

The quiet multiplier, and the one route not built out yet. The event agencies pulled for you each sit on a roster of corporate clients, so one referral relationship can keep sending work our way rather than paying per contact the way cold does.

A handful of those relationships is worth more than a large cold list. It is the route with the most upside still untapped.

Ready to turn on
The Named-Account Layer

Your 12 named accounts, plus 14 similar companies we sourced, are the £20k-plus-a-year targets that sit across all three routes. Each comes with a named decision-maker and the research behind them, worked by name rather than by list. You are running the 12 through HubSpot with that research behind you.

This is the sharpest end of the goal: the exact repeat-buying accounts the whole engagement is pointed at.

What Protects the Pipeline

None of the routes above matter if the sending quietly degrades. Four things run in the background to stop that.

Uptime

Sender monitoring

A daily health sweep catches a disconnected mailbox or a stalled reply within hours. When the warm sender dropped after a login change, it was found the same afternoon and back the next day, so the campaign lost about a day rather than weeks of silence.

Live
Hygiene

List verification

Every list is run through address verification and security-gateway screening before it loads, which is what has kept bounces under the safety line all month.

Live
Capacity

Inbound headroom

A second enquiry address is warming for the September peak, so the daily follow-up volume never rides on one inbox at the busiest time of year.

Warming
Trust

Domain anti-spoofing

The sending domains are being tightened so nobody else can send email that looks like it comes from your address, which otherwise drags down how your genuine mail is treated. The Christmas cold domain is locked down; the corporate domain is next.

Where Things Stand Right Now

The Christmas follow-up pipeline sends every customer email through a single Gmail mailbox: enquire@christmasofficeparty.co.uk, attached to your Google Workspace, orchestrated by Make.com.

For each new inquiry, that mailbox sends:

  • One initial acknowledgement email (immediately, from A1)
  • Up to three follow-up emails (24 to 96 hours apart, from A3)
  • Plus an internal handoff alert to gurmej@ + enquire@ when the lead scores hot

So a single inquiry produces roughly 4 to 6 emails out of that mailbox over a 7 to 10 day window.

Current inquiry pace (last 7 days, measured from MySQL ID delta): around 6.7 inquiries per day, producing roughly 30 to 40 outbound emails per day from the mailbox.

The full inquiries database has 137 active rows in MySQL today. Older 2025 inquiries appear to have been cleaned out of the database, so a precise 2025 peak-month forecast cannot be reconstructed from MySQL alone. See What We Need From You.

The Cliffs

Three different limits sit between today's setup and a "system can't keep up" outcome. They hit at very different volumes, and the first one to hit isn't the one most people think of.

Cliff 1, Gmail spam-filter heuristics on volume spikes Soft, hits first

This isn't a published limit. It's a reputation system. Gmail watches the rate-of-change of any sender, not just absolute volume. A mailbox going from 30 emails per day to 150 emails per day in a single week behaves to Gmail's spam filter like a domain that just got compromised, even when the recipients are real and the content is good. The penalty isn't a bounce; it's deliverability quietly dropping into spam folders.

Critically: you do not see this happen. Sends complete normally, the mailbox doesn't hit any error. Two to three weeks later you notice the conversion rate has tanked because your initial acknowledgement is landing in spam.

Realistic trigger range: anywhere from a 3x to 10x volume jump within 1 to 2 weeks against a baseline like Meji's current 30 per day. So somewhere between 90 per day and 300 per day, depending on how cleanly the ramp-up is executed.

Cliff 2, Google Workspace daily send limit Hard, well-defined

Google Workspace caps a single user mailbox at 2,000 messages per rolling 24-hour window.

For Meji's pattern (4 to 6 emails per inquiry), that means roughly 330 to 500 inquiries per day would saturate the mailbox. On hit, sends stop for 24 hours, then resume.

This cliff is well-documented and recoverable, but it's also a hard wall: there's no warning, the day you cross it you have leads not getting acknowledged.

Cliff 3, Bulk-sender threshold and Google's 2024 enforcement Regulatory, far away

If enquire@christmasofficeparty.co.uk ever sends 5,000+ messages per day to Gmail recipients, it gets permanently classified as a "bulk sender" under Google's 2024 rules. That brings mandatory SPF + DKIM + DMARC alignment, one-click unsubscribe, a hard 0.30% spam-complaint cap, and active enforcement (rejections, not just spam-folder placement) since November 2025.

This designation is permanent once triggered. You don't drop out of bulk-sender status by sending less afterwards.

For Meji's inquiry-driven flow this cliff is far off; you'd need around 830 inquiries per day to cross it. Not a current concern, but worth knowing exists. More relevant to the Instantly outbound side where send volumes are deliberately high.

Three Paths Forward

These aren't mutually exclusive. Most likely you walk through them in sequence as volume actually grows. Pricing is rough and excludes my time; I can sharpen any of these once you've picked a direction.

A
Stay on current setup, add monitoring

Keep the Gmail-via-Make pipeline as-is. Add three things:

  1. Test-recipient virgin domains. A couple of fresh, never-used Gmail or Workspace addresses that get every send as a BCC. Once a week we manually check whether the test recipients see the email in inbox vs spam. This is the first place deliverability drift shows up.
  2. Volume + ops alerting. Automated weekly summary of sends per day, Make ops usage, and any Gmail-side rejection responses.
  3. SPF / DKIM / DMARC audit on christmasofficeparty.co.uk. Overdue from late March. Confirms the mailbox is fully authenticated, which materially improves how Gmail treats it. This is Anuj-side, an hour of his time.

When this is enough: if the Gurmej-anchored projection holds (+10% growth, peak under 20 inquiries per day).

When this isn't enough: the moment you see Jess's June/July ramp materialise above around 50 per day sustained, Cliff 1 starts to bite and we move to Path B.

Cost shape: monitoring is one-time setup, low recurring overhead. Auth audit is essentially free.

B
Multi-mailbox rotation

Add 1 to 2 more sender mailboxes (hello@, events@, or per-venue addresses), rotate sends across them in Make. Each mailbox stays under both Cliff 1's reputation thresholds and Cliff 2's hard cap. Paid for by Workspace per-seat billing.

Trade-offs:

  • Spreads volume so all mailboxes look "natural" to Gmail's reputation system
  • Modest ongoing cost (each new mailbox is a paid Workspace seat)
  • Adds rotation logic in Make, modest one-time build
  • Customer experience: replies from hello@ vs enquire@ look slightly different. Resolvable with reply-to forwarding into one inbox.

When this is right: if Jess's June/July ramp turns into sustained 50 to 200 inquiries per day. Buys roughly 12+ months of headroom.

Cost shape: Workspace seats × N (Google's published rates) plus a one-time Make rotation build.

C
Dedicated outbound infrastructure

Move bulk transactional and outbound sending off the consumer-facing Workspace domain entirely, onto dedicated sender domains warmed up specifically for outreach (the kind of setup that already exists on the cold-outreach side -- around 10-11 warmed domains, per the Gurmej call).

For Meji, this could mean:

  • Christmas follow-up sends move to a parallel domain such as mail.christmasofficeparty.co.uk or a dedicated cousin domain
  • The customer-facing enquire@ mailbox stays for 1:1 conversation only, no automated bulk sends from it
  • Replies still land in enquire@ via reply-to header

Trade-offs:

  • Highest setup effort. Sender domain warm-up takes 4 to 6 weeks before it can carry full volume.
  • Lowest per-email cost at scale.
  • Effectively unbounded headroom against all three cliffs.
  • Decision-coupled with the Instantly outbound work. If that scope materialises, sharing infrastructure across both flows makes sense.

When this is right: sustained 200+ per day, OR you decide to consolidate Christmas + Instantly outbound onto the same dedicated infrastructure for operational simplicity.

Cost shape: dedicated-infra subscription (Instantly tier or comparable) plus warm-up period plus Make-side reconfiguration.

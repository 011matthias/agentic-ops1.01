## What We Understood

Filip, you're expanding your meditation app to five new markets (UK, EU, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand), and you need your privacy policy and terms of service to comply with each region's regulations, especially GDPR.

You already have AI-generated drafts of both documents, which tells us something important: you're comfortable using technology for this kind of work. You're not looking for someone to write these from scratch. You need validation against regulatory requirements, and you need it across multiple frameworks.

The standard approach is hiring a lawyer for a one-time review. That works for a snapshot. But here's the recurring problem: every time you add a new feature, change how you collect data, or a regulation updates (and GDPR enforcement guidance updates regularly), your reviewed documents are outdated. For a solo founder managing five jurisdictions, that review cycle becomes a cost center.

Your meditation app also raises specific compliance questions that generic legal templates miss. If the app tracks meditation sessions, mood, or sleep patterns, that data may qualify as health-related under GDPR Article 9; a special category with stricter processing requirements. Push notification consent requirements differ between UK and Canadian law. Cross-border data transfer documentation is something most apps miss entirely.

## Our Proposed Solution

We're not applying as GDPR lawyers. We build automation tools. What we've put together is a compliance scanning system that does the analytical work, the part that takes a lawyer 15-20 hours, in minutes.

### Concept 1: GDPR Compliance Scanner

A reusable tool that reviews your existing ToS and privacy policy against regulatory checklists for all five of your target markets:

- **Input:** Your ToS and privacy policy text (paste or upload)
- **Process:** AI-powered clause-by-clause analysis against structured regulatory frameworks; UK GDPR, EU GDPR, Canadian PIPEDA, Australian Privacy Act, NZ Privacy Act 2020
- **Output:** Flagged sections with specific regulatory references, risk ratings (high/medium/low), and suggested rewording per flag

What makes this different from your current AI-generated drafts: the scanner uses structured regulatory checklists, not general-purpose AI. It knows the specific articles, the enforcement precedents, and the meditation-app-specific edge cases (Article 9 health data, push notification consent variations, app store privacy label requirements).

And here's the key difference from a one-time legal review: you can run this every time your app changes. New feature? Run the scan. New market? Add the regulatory checklist. Regulation updates? Update the checklist once, scan again. No billable hours.

When you do sit down with a lawyer for final sign-off, the scanner's report cuts their work from 20 hours to 2-3 hours. They're reviewing flagged items, not reading from scratch.

### Concept 2: Prospect & Tester Finder

We noticed you have three other open jobs looking for virtual assistants, Android testers, and meditation app feedback participants. That's a user acquisition challenge that automation handles well:

- Automated outreach to meditation and mindfulness communities
- Qualified lead pipeline with response tracking
- Personalized follow-up sequences

This is a separate engagement if you're interested, but we mention it because it addresses a pattern we see across your open positions.

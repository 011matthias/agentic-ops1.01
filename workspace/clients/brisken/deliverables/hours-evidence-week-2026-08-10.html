How to read this evidence

All of my work for Brisken is software work, and software work leaves a paper trail that cannot be edited after the fact. Every finished piece is submitted to GitHub, the online service (owned by Microsoft) where the project's files are stored. Each submission gets a number and a date-and-time stamp. The stamp is written by GitHub's servers at the moment the submission is accepted; it is stored on their side, and neither I nor anyone else can change it afterwards.

That gives you two separate records to compare. The timesheet was written by me. The timestamps were written by GitHub. If the timesheet were padded, you would see billed blocks with no matching submissions, or stamps at times I never claimed.

Under each day below there is a screenshot of GitHub's own search page, filtered to exactly that day's window (08:00 to 04:00 the next morning). The filter text is visible in the search box of each screenshot, the result count is GitHub's, and the identical page is one click away via the link printed beneath it. The project is publicly viewable, so these pages open for anyone, no account needed.

Why these numbers can be trusted

All of my work for Brisken is software work, and software work leaves a paper trail that cannot be edited after the fact. Every finished piece is submitted to GitHub, the online service (owned by Microsoft) where the project's files are stored. Each submission gets a number and a date-and-time stamp. The stamp is written by GitHub's servers at the moment the submission is accepted; it is stored on their side, and neither I nor anyone else can change it afterwards.

That gives you two separate records to compare. The timesheet below was written by me. The timestamps were written by GitHub. If the timesheet were padded, you would see billed blocks with no matching submissions, or stamps at times I never claimed. Instead, every working day last week has GitHub stamps inside or right at the end of the billed blocks.

The reverse check holds as well: Monday and Wednesday carry no billed hours, and GitHub shows no project activity on either day.

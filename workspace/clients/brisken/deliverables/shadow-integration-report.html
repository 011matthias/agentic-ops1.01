What we found

The headline is the 71%. Across 41 US SAP-treasury job ads, 29 describe staff who build or maintain the interfaces between external systems and SAP, or who do the data work by hand: loading rates, keying trades, reconciling, fixing payment files. That is the shadow integration in plain sight, written into the job description.

The 34% migration share is the timing read, not a second version of the headline. One in three of these roles sits inside an active S/4HANA move, and a migration is the one event that forces a treasury team to re-plumb its data feeds. The hand-built integration that "works for now" becomes a decision the moment the platform underneath it changes.

The 22% vendor figure is the soft one, and we publish it only as a floor. Outside the big consultancies, in-house ads rarely name the market-data or trading vendor in the post even when the team clearly uses one; a skills line reading "Bloomberg preferred" is the usual form. So 22% is the share that names the vendor in the ad text, not the share that uses one, which is far higher.

How we measured it

Population: public US job ads for SAP corporate-treasury, TRM, cash-management and FSCM roles, across in-house, consulting, government and staffing employers. We excluded non-SAP FX-trader roles, market-data-ops roles with no SAP, and general SAP-finance roles with no treasury scope. Each ad was read in full and coded on three yes/no signals.

SignalCoded yes when the ad
Integration / manualdescribes building or maintaining interfaces between external systems and SAP, or manual data work (loads, entry, reconciliation, spreadsheets, payment-file fixes)
Migrationreferences an S/4HANA migration, an ECC-to-S/4 move, or an SAP implementation or transformation
Vendor namednames a specific market-data or trading vendor whose feed must reach SAP (Bloomberg, Refinitiv/LSEG, 360T, FXall, FX GO, CME, OANDA, ICE)

Evidence standard: every coded "yes" is backed by a quoted phrase from the ad. Nothing was inferred without a quote. Every row carries a real source URL; the full dataset with sources is the Shadow Integration Benchmark.

What this number is not

A benchmark is only worth as much as the caveats it concedes up front:

  • The sample carries some consulting weight. Big consultancies templatize the full stack, which pulls the averages up; an in-house-only cut would read lower, especially on the vendor figure. We added in-house corporates and staffing roles specifically to reduce that skew, and the headline fell from an earlier 81% to 71% as we did, which is the honest direction.
  • The vendor-named 22% is a floor, not a usage rate. Treat it as "at least this many name the vendor in the ad."
  • About 27% of rows (11 of 41) were coded from a search-result snippet of the live or cached ad, because the job board was JavaScript-only or access-walled. No code was inferred from a snippet without a quoted phrase.
  • Some sectors returned no quotable US ad on fetchable boards (energy and utility in-house treasury sat behind walled internal systems). Absence of a quotable ad is a coverage gap, not a measured zero.

How common is manual integration work in SAP treasury roles?
In a read of 41 US SAP-treasury job ads, 71% described building or maintaining integrations between external systems and SAP, or doing the manual data work (data loads, manual entry, reconciliation, spreadsheets, payment-file fixes). What is a shadow integration in SAP treasury?
A shadow integration is any data feed into SAP that someone built by hand and no one maintains: a custom market-data script, a spreadsheet upload of rates, a manual trade re-key, a payment-file fix. It works until a vendor changes a field or the author leaves. The job-ad evidence shows this is the default state, not the exception.

What it means for treasury teams

If most SAP-treasury roles are still defined by hand-built integrations, then the integration itself, not the strategy work, is where the team's time goes. That is the case for treating the plumbing as something you buy governed rather than rebuild per feed: a market-data hub that ingests a provider once and distributes it into SAP TRM with an audit trail, or trade capture that lands a venue deal in SAP without the re-key. The migration window is when that choice is cheapest to make, because the feeds are already being rebuilt.

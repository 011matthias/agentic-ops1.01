Getting bank fee statements into SAP, whatever format the bank sends.

CAMT.086 is the ISO 20022 bank-fee-statement format that replaces TWIST BSB. SAP added native bank-fee analysis in S/4HANA 1809 via a Fiori app, but it expects clean CAMT.086 in; banks still send proprietary and TWIST formats. A bank-fee portal ingests any format, enriches for analytics, and distributes to the analyzer, so the fee review is not gated on format.

How do I detect bank overcharges or fee leakage with SAP treasury?
Bank fee statements are rarely audited line by line, so contracted rates and applied charges drift apart unnoticed. Loading the statements into SAP's bank-fee analysis as structured data lets the system compare charged against expected fees and flag discrepancies. The portal's job is getting every statement, in any format, into that analysis so the comparison can run at all.

Where the native path stops, and where the portal starts

SAP's native bank-fee analysis is capable once the data is in: in S/4HANA 1809 it reads CAMT.086 statements through a Fiori app and compares charged fees against expected ones. The limit is not the analysis, it is the front door. Banks have not standardized on CAMT.086; many still send TWIST BSB or a proprietary layout, and those statements never reach the analyzer, so the fees on them never get reviewed. A bank-fee portal sits in front and accepts every format.

The fees that leak are the ones that never get loaded

Fee leakage is not usually a single dramatic overcharge; it is the slow drift between a contracted rate and what the bank actually applies, on the statements nobody reviews because they came in the wrong format. The bank that sends a proprietary file is the bank whose fees go unchecked. Getting that statement into SAP's analysis is what makes the discrepancy visible, which is why the format problem and the leakage problem are the same problem.

You cannot audit a fee statement you never managed to load. Format coverage is overcharge coverage.

What it means for the fee review

The choice is not whether to use SAP's bank-fee analysis; it is whether every bank's statement reaches it. A review that only covers the banks already on CAMT.086 misses exactly the off-format statements where unchecked fees accumulate. Putting a format-agnostic portal in front turns "the banks we happen to get clean files from" into "every bank," which is the difference between a fee audit and a partial one.

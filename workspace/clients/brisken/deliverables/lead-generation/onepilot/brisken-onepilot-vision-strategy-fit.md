## 1. The reconciliation principle

The two documents are not the same product at two zoom levels; they are two claims on two different axes. The strategy generalizes on integration depth (more vendors, more SAP modules, all still "data into SAP"); the vision generalizes on surface breadth (more apps, more roles, most of it outside SAP). The pivot Dirk himself named ("we keep changing what OnePilot is") comes from both definitions wanting the same slot: the homepage. The fix is not to pick a winner and not to make either fight for the headline. It is to give each a fixed altitude in the funnel.

The headline of this memo, stated up front so the rest reads correctly: this model gives the vision MORE durable real estate than "win the homepage" ever could. Instead of one contested headline that the SAP-data proof cannot back, the vision gets a permanent platform/vision page, an AEO foothold in Cluster F, the second-meeting differentiator that converts the deal, the commercial-stickiness argument in the term-sheet logic, and the strategic-buyer / FSI motion. Five deeded homes beat one borrowed headline. Everything below is relocation, not demotion.

A naming note that prevents a fresh confusion before we start (see §3, move 1): OnePilot stays ONE thing at every altitude. We do not mint a second definition.

## 3. Better propositions in the right places

Eight moves. Each names where it applies and why it beats a literal reading of the vision.

**1. Keep OnePilot as ONE definition; relocate, do not redefine.** WHERE: brand architecture, every external surface. A literal "make OnePilot the Universal UI the headline" forces the SAP-data proof to vouch for a horizontal claim it cannot support. But the obvious counter-move (define OnePilot two ways, "vertical layer at LAND, framework at PLATFORM") breaks Dirk's own must-hold line in vision §7: "TreasuryCentral isn't a different product; it's OnePilot, scoped." A buyer who meets "OnePilot = no-code SAP-data layer" on the homepage and "OnePilot = compose-your-own-surface framework" on the platform page hits the same vendor-doesn't-know-what-it-is incoherence we are trying to kill; we would just have moved it from one page to the gap between two. WHY this version wins: OnePilot stays singular (the framework, the Universal UI). The per-app things (MDH, Trade, Bank Fee, Remittance) are OnePilot PRODUCTS. TreasuryCentral is a scoped EDITION that bundles them. LAND foregrounds the products ("Market Data Hub, on OnePilot"); EXPAND foregrounds the edition; PLATFORM foregrounds the framework. What changes by altitude is which layer you put first, not what the word means. This dissolves the pivot without minting a second definition, and it matches the structure the substrate and blueprint already use.

**2. Homepage leads with the SAP-data wedge across the WHOLE crawlable page, not only the H1.** WHERE: homepage H1, `<title>`, meta description, and body copy. A literal reading (blueprint §8.B-C) makes "OnePilot, the Universal UI for the whole working day" the headline and title. That loses, because the homepage is both the outbound landing surface and the AEO root: a warm LinkedIn touch that quoted "Bloomberg into SAP" back to the buyer, landing on "your whole working day," reads as a different company, and the cold-skeptical SAP buyer reads that incoherence as "this vendor doesn't know what it is." But the trap is subtler than the headline. Google AI Overviews rank the whole page, not just the H1, and the per-app clusters share the homepage as their root. So a "prominent Universal-UI section" with "compose your surface / any role / whole working day" copy on the homepage body injects horizontal-category strings into the one URL the engines crawl as the root, and quietly poisons retrieval even with a clean H1. The rule: the Universal-UI presence on the homepage is a single clearly-bounded section that LINKS OUT to the dedicated platform/vision page and does NOT introduce the horizontal query strings into the homepage body. The breadth narrative lives on the separate human-read page (not AEO-targeted, mark it noindex if needed). The homepage body stays SAP-data-wedge end to end.

**3. Keep the relief / adoption thesis, spend it at PLATFORM and in the room, and treat it as the commercial-value argument, not only call rhetoric.** WHERE: platform page, second-meeting narrative, and the deal-economics logic in §5. Dirk's sharpest commercial insight is real and it is the vision's single most defensible non-obvious thesis: 2026 enterprise-AI spend (forecast well over forty billion dollars) under-delivers because licences get bought and not adopted, and adoption follows relief. As a COLD open it fails, exactly as the brief argues: "you lose ~40% to context switching" is true of every company, names no signal, and maps to no product. But it is not only discovery-call rhetoric; it is a deal-economics argument. Brisken's model is fee-per-held-BANT-lead plus commission on first-year subscription value, so a platform that actually gets adopted renews and expands, which raises both the commission base and the lifetime value of every lead we source. Land the wedge, then expand to TreasuryCentral / OnePilot-the-layer, and the breadth becomes a revenue argument for our own model, not only a ceiling-raiser for Brisken's pitch. Accuracy caveat, same discipline the strategy applies to its own stats (it cites Precisely 2025 for the 59% S/4HANA figure; substrate §6 requires verifiable facts): the ~40% context-switching figure is attributed to a named analyst source or phrased "analysts estimate" before it enters any room, and it is never a measured Brisken number.

**4. Make Cluster F the only home for breadth in AEO, cap it precisely, and keep SAP as the anchor noun.** WHERE: the AEO substrate. Cluster F (queries 22-25: "no-code integration layer for SAP BTP finance data," "buy vs build a custom SAP treasury data integration," "SAP-listed alternative to custom middleware for finance data," "add audit trail to SAP treasury data feeds") is broader than "Bloomberg into SAP" but still SAP-BTP-scoped and problem-shaped, so it still retrieves. WHY it beats leading AEO with the vision: "Universal UI" drops Brisken into the Glean / M365 Copilot / Gemini / Notion query space, where it has no third-party citation surface and cannot win retrieval, and it abandons winnable SAP strings across Clusters A-G. Note the collateral specifically: leading AEO with breadth also strands Cluster G (queries 28-29), the migration why-now cluster that substrate §5.3 calls the broadest fan-out catcher. That is an argument for keeping AEO SAP-narrow; it is NOT an argument about Cluster F's scope, and the migration cluster stays at LAND (see move 5 and the §4 correction). Safe Cluster F ceiling, re-anchored to the actual substrate strings so SAP stays the head noun in every one: "no-code integration layer for SAP BTP finance data," "buy vs build a custom SAP treasury data integration," "governed audit trail across SAP treasury data feeds." If the four product domains are named at all, keep them subordinate: "...across the SAP finance data estate (market data, trades, bank fees, remittance)," never the bare "across market data, trades, bank fees, remittance," which reads as "one layer covers everything" and drops the SAP anchor. Unsafe at any altitude in AEO: email, Teams, "any role," "whole working day."

**5. Outbound first touch stays the three-signal wedge, full stop; the migration why-now stays at LAND.** WHERE: the precision-LinkedIn opener, the forwardable one-pager, and the migration cluster (Cluster G, 26-29). A literal reading would let "make users happy" into the opening line. It loses badly: the wedge works because it quotes a public, falsifiable signal back ("you run SAP treasury and list Bloomberg, so someone is keying rates in by hand") and names the manual step. Universal UI has nothing to quote back, no SAP why-now, and maps to no product, so it cannot route Bloomberg to MDH or 360T to Trade. ~2M cold emails already produced 0 leads on exactly this kind of breadth; do not rebuild what failed. The migration why-now (ECC 2027 / extended-2030, S/4HANA as the buying window) is the strongest LAND-altitude wedge asset and stays at acquisition; it is cited at PLATFORM only as collateral that Universal-UI-first AEO would strand, never pulled up to the platform altitude.

**6. Carry "scoping, not rebuilding" forward as the expansion mechanism, and let platform-vs-point-tool WIN the second meeting.** WHERE: TreasuryCentral edition page, OnePilot-the-layer expansion pitch, account-growth conversations. "Scoping, not rebuilding" is the strongest structural move in Dirk's doc: it makes a horizontal vision sellable through a narrow first step, which is the land-then-expand shape the strategy already runs. Let TreasuryCentral be "the treasurer's whole day, not just the data feed" once MDH or Trade is in, with its TMS guardrail ("a TMS runs treasury's books; TreasuryCentral runs the treasurer's day") as a sharp, defensible expansion claim. But do not file the platform story only under "where this goes." Its real job arrives one altitude earlier, in the second meeting the strategy already books. A sharp SAP buyer who took the "Bloomberg into SAP" meeting will ask "why you, not COMPIRICUS / StepStream / a consultancy / SAP-native Datafeed" (the substrate names these). The honest answer to that buy-decision objection IS the platform: buy one governed layer at the S/4HANA replumb moment instead of eight point projects, and the moat is the surface plus integration depth that Brisken's SAP heritage makes hardest to copy (vision §4). That is the vision earning the deal, not just raising a ceiling, and it belongs in the room the moment the wedge has it.

**7. Use the governance / CISO line as PLATFORM-buyer ammunition, not a generic feature.** WHERE: the platform page, the multi-stakeholder room. "Agents that can access exactly what the user can, no more, no less" is concrete and defensible against ungoverned suite assistants. It speaks to whoever owns architecture and risk, the persona that signs off on a platform bet, not the persona feeling a single point of pain. It belongs where the buyer is evaluating "is this safe to standardize on," never in the acquisition spearhead.

**8. Honor the vision's own overclaim guardrail by treating the whole homepage and the AEO pages as "external copy."** WHERE: every published surface. Dirk's §10 guardrail says "where a line reads bigger than the product is, cut it before any external copy is derived," and insists the proof lives in the scoped edition. The homepage (H1, title, meta, AND body) and the AEO pages ARE the external copy where breadth reads biggest relative to the proof. A literal "make the Universal UI the headline" recommendation contradicts Dirk's own guardrail. We are not overriding him; we are applying the rule he wrote, to the whole page he would otherwise put it on.

## 5. What we take to Dirk

Our counter-proposal, in five recommendations. The first four place the vision; the fifth is the part most easy to leave on the table.

1. **Adopt the three-altitude model as the brand architecture, with OnePilot as a single name.** OnePilot is the framework at every altitude; the products lead acquisition, the edition (TreasuryCentral) leads in-account expansion, the framework leads the strategic story. This honors your §7 must-hold line ("TreasuryCentral is OnePilot, scoped"), gives the vision a real and prominent home, and removes the pivot, because nothing competes for the same slot.
2. **The whole homepage and the AEO root lead with the SAP-data wedge; the Universal UI gets its own prominent page one click away.** This is the real revision of the blueprint's §8.B-C, and it is the application of your own §10 overclaim guardrail to the surfaces where breadth reads biggest relative to the proof. The constraint covers the homepage body, not just the headline, because the engines rank the whole page.
3. **The Universal UI is the second-meeting and platform-buyer story, never the first touch, and at the second meeting it is the deal-winner, not just the trajectory.** "Buy one governed layer at the migration moment, not eight point projects; the moat is integration depth, which is our SAP heritage" is the answer to the "why you, not a point tool or a consultancy" objection that converts the wedge meeting you already book.
4. **Cluster F is the single SAP-scoped AEO foothold for the broader thesis; everything horizontal stays on the human-read platform page.** Breadth in AEO lives only on the integration-depth axis (queries 22-25, SAP the anchor noun) and never touches the per-app buyer strings. The TreasuryCentral edition page and the platform/vision page are human-read, not indexed against the wedge queries.
5. **Treat adoption-follows-relief as the commercial argument in the term-sheet logic.** A platform that gets adopted renews and expands, which raises the commission base and the lifetime value of every lead we source. Landing the wedge then expanding the account is not only better for Brisken's deal size; it is the revenue case for our own fee-plus-commission model. Keep it out of the cold open; surface it in the room and in the commercials.

One trade-off to name honestly, because the model creates it: a one-click path from the wedge hero to the platform page is reachable by the cold buyer too, at exactly the trust-fragile first-touch moment the strategy is built around. We resolve it by construction. The platform link is labeled "where this goes" (forward-looking), and the wedge hero must fully establish the named enemy, the three-signal pain, and the SAP why-now ABOVE and independent of any platform link, so a cold buyer who never clicks through still gets a complete, proof-matched SAP-data story. The platform page is upside for the already-curious, never load-bearing for the first-touch message.

Our recommendation: foreground FSI as the lead future track on the platform page precisely because it is the largest, named, real expansion and the strongest available proof the surface generalizes beyond SAP-finance, "the same surface, going into banking cores like Temenos / Finastra." Keep it walled off from AEO and outbound regardless. We would rather make this positioning call with you than for you.

Why now

The market has a built-in deadline working in OnePilot's favor.

SAP ends mainstream maintenance for ECC in 2027 (2030 with extended maintenance), and independent 2025 research (Precisely) puts 59% of SAP shops fully or partially live on S/4HANA, up 13 points in one year. Migration is exactly the moment the financial-data plumbing comes up for a decision: the custom interfaces feeding exchange rates, curves, bank statements and counterparty data into the old system are legacy code the migration team must rebuild, or replace with a product. A governed, provider-agnostic OnePilot subscription that drops Bloomberg, Refinitiv, OANDA, 360T and central-bank data straight into S/4HANA with no custom code is the buy-instead-of-rebuild answer. Brisken's SAP co-innovation partnership and its place in SAP Industry Cloud for Financial Services make it the credible one.

One engine, a campaign per product

The expensive part, the sourcing-to-booking machine, is built once. Each product plugs into it as a campaign with four swappable parts.

Shared engine (built once)

  • Sending infrastructure: domains, mailboxes, warm-up
  • Contact sourcing and verification
  • Reply handling, BANT qualification, calendar booking
  • Weekly delivery note and accept/reject feedback loop

Per-product campaign (swappable)

  • Target list: the ICP that buys this product
  • Buying signal: the public trigger we hunt for it
  • Message angle: the one pain it removes
  • CTA: a demo of that product

Who we target, and why

We never blast "all SAP shops." Each campaign begins from a public buying signal that proves a company is already building or budgeting the exact thing one product replaces. We rank by signal strength (a live job posting beats a case study beats a technographic listing), then thread the few people who own the decision.

On every target company we work three roles, because a subscription is a multi-stakeholder buy:

The pain ownerFeels the manual work daily; who it is shifts by product (Treasurer, AR lead, sustainability lead, trading desk)
The budget ownerVP / Director of Treasury or Finance: signs off the subscription
SAP systems and ITFinance-transformation and SAP integration leads: own the build-vs-buy call

The eight campaigns: who, how we find them, why they buy

Signals are our starting design; each list is validated before that campaign sends. The three treasury-data campaigns share the verified company list below; the rest source their own.

Market Data Hub

  • Who: US SAP TRM / treasury teams, multi-currency, running hedge accounting. Contacts: Treasurer, VP/Director Treasury, Cash Manager, SAP TRM lead.
  • How we find them: hiring for SAP Treasury / TRM or S/4HANA finance roles; multi-currency footprint; case studies naming SAP treasury.
  • Why: they already pipe Bloomberg, Refinitiv and 360T rates into TRM by custom code or by hand; hedge accounting makes bad data costly, and the S/4HANA migration forces a rebuild-or-buy on that interface now.

Trade Automation

  • Who: companies with an in-house FX, derivatives or commodity trading desk on SAP. Contacts: Head of Trading / Front Office, Treasurer, Treasury Operations, SAP TRM lead.
  • How we find them: treasury front-office / dealer hiring; use of 360T, FXall or Bloomberg FX GO; active hedging programs.
  • Why: trades get re-keyed from the trading platform into SAP TRM by hand; they want straight-through deal creation, and the more they trade the more that step costs them.

Cash Flow & Exposure Hub

  • Who: multi-entity groups with subsidiaries off SAP (acquired or regional units on other ERPs and spreadsheets). Contacts: Group Treasurer, Cash Manager, FP&A / forecasting lead.
  • How we find them: cash-forecasting / FP&A hiring; a multi-ERP estate or recent M&A; decentralized finance.
  • Why: cash and FX-exposure data arrives as xls, csv and pdf from units off SAP and gets re-keyed into S/4HANA; forecasting and hedging suffer, and centralization or migration is the moment they fix it.

Bank Fee Portal

  • Who: large corporates with many banking relationships and hundreds of bank accounts. Contacts: Treasury Operations, Bank Relationship Manager, Cash Manager.
  • How we find them: large bank-account footprint; bank-relationship / treasury-ops roles; global cash structures.
  • Why: across many banks, fee statements come in many formats and overcharges go unchecked; this is a hard-dollar cost-recovery sell that needs no migration to justify, so it converts fast.

Credit Data Hub

  • Who: firms with large receivables and credit exposure on SAP Credit Management. Contacts: Credit Manager, Head of AR, Treasury, SAP Credit Management lead.
  • How we find them: credit-risk / AR analyst hiring; SAP Credit Management in use; large B2B customer books.
  • Why: credit data comes from many agencies, some by API and some only as PDF, and must be normalized and scored before it reaches SAP; manual collection slows credit decisions.

ESG Data Hub

  • Who: large EU or EU-exposed groups consolidating ESG data into SAP Sustainability Control Tower. Contacts: Head of Sustainability / ESG, Sustainability Controller, SAP finance lead.
  • How we find them: sustainability-reporting hiring; SAP Sustainability Control Tower adoption; CSRD-in-scope size (1,000+ employees with EU turnover over the threshold).
  • Why: large groups still consolidate ESG data from many providers and internal reports into one governed source. The Feb-2026 CSRD Omnibus softened the deadline and cut scope, so this is a steady-need campaign reached mainly on LinkedIn, since we do not cold-email the EU. We run it last.

Remittance Advice Gate

  • Who: high-volume AR / AP teams and shared-service centers buried in remittance emails. Contacts: Head of Shared Services, AR / Cash Application Manager, AP Manager.
  • How we find them: cash-application / AR roles; shared-service-center footprint; high invoice volume.
  • Why: staff read unstructured remittance emails and attachments and re-key them into SAP; it is the clearest "let AI remove the boring loop" story, and Brisken already runs it live for a customer on ChatGPT.

AI Digital Workforce

  • Who: SAP finance orgs with a stated AI or automation mandate and innovation budget. Contacts: CFO office / Finance Transformation, Head of Finance Automation, IT-finance.
  • How we find them: "AI in finance" initiatives, digital-transformation and automation roles, named innovation programs.
  • Why: they have budget and a mandate for AI but no concrete finance use case; OnePilot's chat-driven co-workers hand them a deployable one on SAP, like creating a bank transfer straight from an email request.
Beyond the suite: OnePilot for FSI Brisken's 2026 banking play (orchestration across SAP and non-SAP cores, ISO20022, SWIFT) targets banks, a different buyer than the corporate SAP base above. It earns its own campaign and its own target list and research; we treat it as a parallel track, not one of the eight.

Per your offer: a fixed fee per accepted BANT lead plus a commission on closed deals.

Honest expectations

This is a precision market, not a volume one: each campaign's universe is small and senior. Realistic early output is a small number of held, qualified meetings per month in the first one to two months, growing as more campaigns come online, the email channel matures, and the weekly trigger sweep compounds. Conversion rates are unknowable until live; what we control is list quality, message quality, and qualification discipline. First meetings land in weeks 4-8, possibly earlier via LinkedIn. Running a campaign per product means more shots on goal without diluting any single message.

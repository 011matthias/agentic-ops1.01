The migration is the cheapest moment to replace hand-built scripts with a governed hub, because the feeds are being rebuilt anyway. The questions a migrating treasury team asks

What treasury data integrations need rebuilding during an ECC-to-S/4HANA migration?
A treasury move to S/4HANA touches every external feed: market-data and rate interfaces, trade capture from FX venues, bank connectivity and payment files, and remittance or cash-application flows. Each custom interface built for ECC has to be re-validated or rebuilt for S/4HANA. The migration is when teams decide whether to re-code them or move onto a governed hub.

How do I avoid re-coding market-data interfaces when moving SAP treasury to S/4HANA?
Re-platform the feed once instead of re-coding per interface. Moving market-data, rate and curve feeds onto a governed hub during the migration keeps the integration in configuration, not ABAP, so the S/4HANA cutover is a re-point rather than a rebuild, and future provider changes do not trigger another code change.

Why now: the buying window has a deadline

SAP's published roadmap ends mainstream maintenance for ECC (Business Suite 7) in 2027, with extended maintenance to 2030, so the install base is moving to S/4HANA on a clock. For treasury that is not a back-office event: it is the one time the external data feeds are reopened. A hand-built market-data script or a manual rate upload that "works for now" stops being invisible the moment the platform under it changes, because someone has to make it work on S/4HANA. That is the decision point: rebuild it as it was, or move it onto a managed interface that does not need rebuilding next time.

The migration is also when AI co-workers go in

A migration reopens the processes, not only the interfaces, which is when teams add a digital workforce rather than re-document the manual steps. The same questions come up.

How do I automate repetitive treasury tasks in SAP without adding headcount?
A digital workforce assigns one AI co-worker per repetitive process, a clerk, an analyst, a specialist, that fetches, summarizes, recommends and executes across SAP and other systems. What it means for the migration plan

The integrations are getting rebuilt either way; the only choice is what they get rebuilt into. Re-coding each feed for S/4HANA recreates the same shadow integrations on a newer platform, and the next provider change starts the cycle again. Moving the feeds onto a governed hub during the cutover spends the migration effort once and removes the rebuild-next-time, which is why the migration window, not a quiet quarter afterwards, is when the decision is cheapest.

Re-plumb once, onto a managed interface, while the feeds are already open.

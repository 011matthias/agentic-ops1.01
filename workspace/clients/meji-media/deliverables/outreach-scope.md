## How we'll work together

You set direction and approve the things that carry risk (cold lists, copy, go-live). I run the build and the week-to-week operation, and I bring you a decision only when it's genuinely yours to make. The weekly update is the heartbeat: same format every Monday, so you always know where things stand without having to ask.

Two rules sit above everything and never bend:

1. **No cold campaign goes live until you have seen and approved a sample of the real contact list.** You check job titles, companies, locations, and relevance yourself before a single email sends.
2. **Every campaign gets a proper follow-up sequence, never a single send.**

These two are the direct fix for what went wrong before. They are not negotiable down, by either of us.

## The seven workstreams

These map one-to-one to the priorities you set out.

### 1. Christmas outreach, rebuilt around your warm database

Your past customers and past enquirers are the strongest audience you have. We rebuild the Christmas outreach around them: clean the list, group people by how recently they engaged, and run a proper sequence. This is the first thing live because the calendar demands it (see Timing below).

### 2. Banter outreach, rebuilt around its warm database

Same approach for Banter, lower volume and a calmer tone. It is the quieter brand and the outreach should feel that way. Plan follows the Christmas one.

### 3. Better cold data for corporate events

The cold side failed before because the data was wrong, not because the system was wrong. We fix the data sourcing properly: compare several contact-data providers (Apollo and alternatives) on the same brief, pull a real sample from each, and you pick the source from samples you can see, not from sales promises.

### 4. Clean Christmas cold data for the party cities

For each city you run parties in (Birmingham, Leicester, Wolverhampton), we build a filtered list: the right job titles, the right company size, within range of the venue, checked for viability before anything goes live. Each city list comes to you as a sample first.

### 5. Sample-data approval before any cold send

This is rule 1 above, operationalised. Before any cold campaign, you get a sample (roughly 100 to 200 contacts) and you flag anything off. Nothing sends until you have said yes.

### 6. A proper follow-up sequence on every campaign

This is rule 2 above. Three touches over roughly ten days, the sequence stops the moment someone replies, and bounced contacts drop out automatically. No more one-shot sends.

### 7. Weekly update, every Monday morning

One short email you can read in 30 seconds: emails sent, bounce rate, replies, positive replies, opportunities or calls booked, and any issues or changes needed. Same format every week so it stays scannable.

## Timing, and why September drives the calendar

We pulled eleven years of your enquiry data. The pattern is consistent every year: enquiries peak hard in September, with August through October running far above the rest of the year. A typical September day brings 20 to 25 enquiries sustained for weeks; the single biggest day on record was 42 (3 September 2025).

What this means for the plan:

- **The Christmas warm rebuild has to be live by mid-July.** That gives the audience four to six weeks of engagement before the inbound peak starts. Launching into the peak itself competes with your own busiest period.
- **Cold campaigns ramp before August or hold during September.** Heavy cold volume in September would strain deliverability exactly when your customer-acknowledgement inbox needs the headroom most.
- **The August-to-October weeks are the high-stakes ones.** The weekly update matters most then.

So the rough order: warm rebuilds and the cold-data evaluation start now, warm goes live by mid-July, cold campaigns follow once their lists are approved, and the system runs steady through the September peak.

## What success looks like

- Warm campaigns performing at or above the levels we already know work (the Christmas and Banter warm audiences have a track record).
- Cold campaigns that send to lists you approved, so they do not bounce-pause the way the previous ones did.
- A Monday update you can act on in 30 seconds, every week, without chasing.
- The September peak handled without a deliverability incident.

## Who decides what

**Your calls:** which cold-data provider we commit to (from the samples), final approval on every cold list, sign-off on sequence copy before it sends, the commercial structure for the ongoing work.

**My calls:** how the lists are cleaned and segmented, the technical build of the sequences, deliverability pacing and mailbox rotation, the day-to-day operation, what goes in the weekly update.

**Shared:** the launch timing for each campaign, and anything where the data tells us something that changes the plan.

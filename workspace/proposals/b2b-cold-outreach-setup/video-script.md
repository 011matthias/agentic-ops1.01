# Video Content Guide: B2B Cold Outreach Setup (p028)

Variant B (new build). Target 2:00 to 2:30. Speak from the bullets in your own
words; the prospect is anonymous, so address them as "you" throughout. Screen
plus camera bubble, start on the work.

## 0:00 Open: four tools, one pipeline, two outputs

- One line of who you are, then straight to their post.
- The reframe to land: they listed four tools, but what they are really buying
  is ONE pipeline with TWO outputs. LinkedIn messages going out now, and a
  validated email list they can run campaigns on later.
- Credibility in passing, not as a pitch: this exact stack is what you run for
  a client today, so everything in the video comes from a live system.

## 0:20 Walk the pipeline on screen

- Have a simple left-to-right flow visible: Sales Navigator, then
  PhantomBuster, then Apollo, then verification, then Instantly.
- One job per tool, in order:
  - Sales Navigator: where the ICP gets precise. Saved searches make the list
    reproducible, not a one-off export.
  - PhantomBuster: runs the LinkedIn side. Exports the search results, sends
    connection requests and DMs on a schedule.
  - Apollo: enriches the same people with verified work emails and company
    data.
  - Verification step: every address tiered deliverable, catch-all, or
    invalid before it is saved. This is what "clean list" actually means.
  - Instantly: the future email channel. Separate sending domains,
    authentication records, warm-up from day one.
- Their phrase to echo here: "how each tool contributes to the workflow".

## 0:55 The honest part: 500 DMs in two weeks

- This is the beat that wins or loses the job. Deliver it as working
  knowledge, not a warning.
- The working numbers from running accounts: roughly 100 connection requests
  and 50 to 80 automated DMs per week per account hold up safely. Past that,
  accounts get restricted, and a restricted account costs more than two extra
  weeks.
- So: if their 500 targets are already first-degree connections, the two-week
  number is realistic at about 35 DMs a day. If the targets are cold, the
  honest plan is phased: connection requests ramp first, DMs follow each
  acceptance, and 500 completes over three to four weeks.
- Frame as likely-cause honesty: you have not seen their LinkedIn account, so
  the exact plan depends on account age and how many matching first-degree
  connections already exist.

## 1:35 Proof, then how you would confirm scope

- One proof block, kept tight: a live client operation today. Three active
  Instantly campaigns on multiple sending domains. Apollo ICP filters built as
  tiered sets and sample-validated on 200 contacts before the full pull.
  Domains and mailboxes stood up from zero, authentication records included.
- Optional depth if it flows: you found and fixed a sequence-timing bug in
  live campaigns by reading the Instantly API spec, the difference between
  configuring a UI and owning a system.
- First things you would ask for to lock scope: their ICP definition and the
  state of the LinkedIn account that will send (age, plan, rough count of
  matching first-degree connections).

## 1:55 Close: price in one line, then the easy question

- Price, stated plainly: $40 an hour, estimated 15 to 25 hours for the full
  setup, so $600 to $1,000 total. Subscriptions run on their own accounts, so
  they own everything.
- End on the one-line question, not a pitch: roughly how many of the 500
  target people are already first-degree connections on the account that will
  send? Their answer scopes the whole LinkedIn phase.
- One take, one ending.

## Terms to gloss if you say them on camera

- ICP: ideal customer profile, the exact people you target
- DM: direct message inside LinkedIn
- First-degree connection: someone already connected to the account
- Sales Navigator: LinkedIn's paid prospect search tool
- PhantomBuster: a tool that automates LinkedIn actions
- Apollo: a contact database for finding verified work emails
- Instantly: a cold email sending platform
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC: the DNS records that prove a domain may send mail
- Warm-up: slowly ramping a new mailbox so providers trust it
- Catch-all: a domain that accepts all mail, so the address is unverifiable
- Enrichment: adding emails and company data to a contact record

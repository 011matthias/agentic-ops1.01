Week one in your Instantly account: what I check

Matthias Neumann · UnpauseAI · November 2026

The audit is read-only: nothing in your account changes during it. At the end of the week you get a short report ranked by impact, with the first two or three changes I'd make. Where a check caught something in the campaigns I ran this year, the number is in bold.

1Deliverability

Every sending account: warmup on, a daily limit set, account healthy, SPF, DKIM and DMARC published on its domain One weak sender drags down the reputation of the domain it shares with the others.
Bounce rate per campaign, split by the recipient's mail server A July batch where every address was NeverBounce "valid" bounced at 8.6%; 10 of the 11 bounces sat behind Mimecast, a security gateway a verifier can't see. With those domains held out, the next two waves bounced at 1.5% and 0%.

2Sequence and sending settings

The delay on each step A step's delay is the wait before the next email. A gap entered on the step it precedes sends the follow-up about 20 minutes after the first email.
Gap between emails and daily limits, against list size An 11-minute gap inherited from an older campaign held sends at 45 a day against 75 expected. Cutting it to 5 minutes brought it to about 72.
Campaigns that already hold a finished run A step added to a completed campaign set it active again and put 533 of its 556 finished leads back in the sequence. A new run goes in a fresh campaign.
Stop on reply, sending window, recipients' time zone The window follows the recipients' time zone, not the sender's. North American and European clocks change on different weekends, so cross-Atlantic schedules get rechecked in March and October.

3Lists

Verification and mail-server type before every load Domains behind Mimecast, or with no mail server at all, are held out before they can bounce.
The same person in two campaigns New leads are deduplicated against the whole workspace, not only the campaign they are going into: the last wave was checked against all 10,544 leads in the account.
Customers, open deals and opt-outs, checked against your own records A re-check just before one launch found 28 new bookings in nine days, one of them in the send list. The stop-list is refreshed every week.
Merge fields Any lead whose first name, company or custom field is empty gets fixed or pulled before it renders as a blank in the email.

4Replies and reporting

Human replies, counted apart from automatic ones Across two cold waves, out-of-office and leave notices outnumbered human replies 27 to 10.
Replies flagged interested, against who actually answered them In one audit, seven interested replies had no confirmed answer from anyone; one asked to reserve 10 seats.
Lead counts by status Instantly's active status also covers leads that were never emailed: 258 of 267 counted as in sequence had not had a first email. Progress is read from the last-contact date.
Open rates Apple Mail's privacy protection loads tracking pixels on its own, so opens are read as a trend, never as interest.

```text
Matthias here. A conference follow-up I ran this year booked meetings with 20 of the 127 people it reached. Attached is a 30-day plan for reaching dealer principals and GMs, including exactly which appointments you'd pay for.

The second attachment is a two-page case study with the numbers below. Your five points, in order:

1) Campaigns I've managed
- For a treasury-software company I ran the follow-up to a conference: 127 people reached, 40 in conversation, 20 with a meeting booked. Every figure is computed from an event log and a daily scan of the mailboxes, not typed into a report.
- For a UK events company I ran Instantly this year. Its corporate cold outreach sent 446 first emails over three waves. The July batch was all NeverBounce "valid" and still bounced 8.6%, with 10 of 11 bounces behind Mimecast, a security gateway a verifier can't see. With a mail-server check at load, the next two waves bounced 1.5% and then 0%. Its re-engagement of 773 past customers bounced 0.6% across three emails.

2) Booking rates
The conference follow-up booked meetings with 16% of the people it reached. On cold email to corporate buyers, 10 of 134 people wrote back over a three-email run (7.5%), not counting 15 automatic replies. Neither list was dealerships, so I won't quote you a dealer booking rate I haven't measured. With per-appointment pricing that risk sits with me, and the weekly report shows the rate on your list from the first week of sending.

3) Tools and infrastructure
Instantly for sending and warmup; Apollo for contacts, checked against each dealership's own staff page; NeverBounce plus a mail-server check before every load; Google Workspace mailboxes on three new domains in Ribit's name, so your main domain never sends cold; n8n to pass booked meetings into your CRM. For Canadian dealers, CASL shapes the list: an address from a data vendor doesn't carry implied consent on its own, while one the dealership publishes itself can, if the email relates to the person's role. Each Canadian contact keeps the page and date it came from.

4) Pricing
I'd propose $200 per qualified appointment that takes place, with a $600 advance at the start that covers the first three and the weeks new mailboxes need to warm up before they can send cold. The plan defines qualified: an approved title (Dealer Principal, owner, GM, General Sales Manager, or a group executive at a dealer group) at a US or Canadian dealership, not already your customer or in your pipeline, and the meeting is held. No-shows aren't billed until they're rescheduled and held. The domains, mailboxes and Instantly workspace are in your name, so they stay with you either way.

5) Launch speed
Starting from zero: domains and mailboxes in the first two days, list and copy to you for approval in week one, first emails in week three, so the first appointments would land in weeks three to four. With warmed mailboxes already in place, sending starts in week one.

I work within UnpauseAI, an EU-based automation consultancy, and do this work myself. I'm in Karlsruhe, Germany, seven hours ahead of Winnipeg, so my afternoons and evenings overlap your working day.

Do you already have sending domains warming up, or would we start from zero?

Matthias
```

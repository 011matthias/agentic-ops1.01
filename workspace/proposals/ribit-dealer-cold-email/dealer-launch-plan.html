Dealer appointments for Ribit: the first 30 days

Matthias Neumann · UnpauseAI · November 2026

How I'd run cold email to dealership owners and managers in the US and Canada, starting with the term a per-appointment deal turns on: what counts as qualified. Nothing sends, loads or changes in a live system without your yes.

1What you pay for

Counts as a qualified appointment

  • A meeting on your calendar that takes place
  • The attendee holds a title you approve before launch, at a US or Canadian dealership: Dealer Principal, owner, GM or General Sales Manager; at a group, the executive over digital, marketing or operations
  • Not a current customer or an open opportunity in your CRM, checked before any list loads

Does not count

  • A no-show, until it is rescheduled and held
  • An attendee below the approved titles
  • A second meeting with the same rooftop
  • Anyone already in your pipeline

$200 per qualified appointment held. A $600 advance at the start covers the first three and the weeks the new mailboxes spend warming up. You have 48 hours after a meeting to flag one that did not qualify, and it comes off the bill. Domains, mailboxes and the Instantly workspace sit in Ribit's name at cost, so the warmed setup and the list stay with you.

2Who we write to

Single-store dealers Dealer Principal or owner, and the GM One store, one decision: the person who signs for software is usually the person reading the email.
Dealer groups The group executive over digital, marketing or operations One conversation can cover every rooftop in the group, so groups get their own list and their own copy.
Second entry point, tested as its own stream BDC manager (the team that answers leads and books showroom visits) or internet sales manager They see the unanswered inquiries Ribit fixes every day. The test is whether they bring a GM into the meeting.

3Where the list comes from

US dealers Apollo, filtered to dealerships and the approved titles, with each contact checked against the dealer's own staff page for a current name and title.
Canadian dealers CASL sets the source. Implied consent by publication covers an address the dealership publishes itself, usually on its staff page, with no notice refusing unsolicited email, and only for an email relevant to that person's role. An address from a data vendor does not qualify on its own. Each Canadian contact keeps the page URL and the date it was found; your counsel signs off on the basis before launch.
Before every load Verification, then a mail-server check: in a July batch where every address was NeverBounce "valid", 10 of 11 bounces sat behind Mimecast, a security gateway a verifier cannot see. Those domains, and any with no mail server, are held out. Then a dedupe against your CRM and every live campaign.

4What we send from

Three new domains in Ribit's name. Your main domain never sends cold email, so its reputation is untouched.

Two Google Workspace mailboxes per domain, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, warmed in Instantly for two to three weeks.

Up to 30 cold emails per mailbox a day: 180 a day, about 300 new contacts a week on three emails each.

Recipient's time zone, Monday to Friday, easing off at month-end, when sales managers chase the month's numbers.

5Timeline, starting from zero · with warmed mailboxes already in place, sending starts in week one

Days 1 to 2Domains, mailboxes and DNS records set up; warmup starts. The title list and the qualified definition agreed with you.
Week 1First list built, about 1,000 contacts. Copy and reply playbook to you for approval; booking link and CRM handoff wired.
Week 2Warmup continues. The list is checked: verification, mail servers, duplicates, and the CASL record on every Canadian row.
Week 3First cold emails, ramping to the full daily cap. Interested replies answered the same day with times or your booking link.
Week 4 onAppointments, the weekly report, and one change at a time, each logged with the number it should move.

6Every week

Per stream (single store, group, BDC): emails sent, bounces by mail server, human replies, automatic replies, meetings booked, held, no-shows, appointments billed, and the one change made that week with the number it should move. Counted from Instantly and your calendar when the report is written, never typed by hand.

# Positioning

## Core Identity

You are not a freelancer looking for jobs. You are a systems thinker and builder who reduces uncertainty for the client immediately.

The client's internal question is always:
> "Can this person actually solve my problem without creating new ones?"

Your job is to answer that before they consciously think it.

## Mental Model

Instead of: "How do I get hired?"
Think: "How do I make this feel already solved?"

## Weak vs Strong

**Weak positioning:**
- "I'm an automation expert with 5 years of experience and I'd love to help you."
- Talks about tools and skills
- Lists experience

**Strong positioning:**
- "I recorded a short walkthrough where I break down your workflow and show how I'd structure it."
- Shows understanding of the specific problem
- Demonstrates decisions and reasoning

## Using Background Strategically

Background is **supporting evidence**, not the main argument.

Clients don't hire "7 years of experience." They hire "this person clearly understands my problem."

### How to reference experience

Bad: "I have experience with automation systems and AI workflows."

Good: "I've worked on systems where reliability actually matters, so I tend to structure flows with validation, fallback handling, and logging from the start."

### The "600k impressions" proof point

Use only when relevant to the job:
- Marketing/analytics job: "I've worked on systems handling ~600k weekly impressions, so I'm used to thinking in terms of data integrity, tracking accuracy, and failure visibility."
- Pure automation job: Skip it entirely. Don't force.

**Rule:** Experience = credibility enhancer, not opener.

## Competition Axis

You are NOT competing on:
- Price
- Proposal length
- Years of experience

You ARE competing on:
- Clarity of understanding
- Speed of comprehension
- Proof of capability

10 applicants say "I can do this." You say "Here's how it works." You win.

## Tone Rules

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Calm, direct | "I'd love to help" |
| Slightly detached | "I'm very passionate" |
| Confident without pushing | "I'm the best" |
| Peer-level conversation | Applicant energy |
| "This is straightforward if structured correctly" | "I'm very excited to work with you" |

## Anti-Patterns

- Opening with credentials or experience
- Listing tools (Zapier, Make, n8n) without context
- Generic intros that could apply to any job
- Overselling or using superlatives
- Asking questions you could answer from the job post
